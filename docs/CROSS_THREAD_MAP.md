**Grounded basics.**
- *Electrolyte / ionic-liquid gating.* An electrolyte or ionic liquid on a material forms an electric double layer about 1 nm thick. A few volts then induce sheet carrier densities of ~10¹⁴–10¹⁵ cm⁻², far beyond solid dielectric gates. Examples: field-induced superconductivity in SrTiO₃ and ZrNCl; metal–insulator switching in VO₂ (partly by electrochemical oxygen-vacancy formation, not purely electrostatic); tuning of graphene and other 2D materials; and ion-intercalation "ionic gating" that changes the phase of oxides. Standard references (Ueno et al., Nakano et al. 2012, Jeong et al. 2013) [to verify exact citations].
- *Biological ion-channel gating.* Channels open and close in response to voltage (Hodgkin–Huxley 1952; voltage-gated Na⁺/K⁺), ligands, mechanical stress (Piezo1/2) or light (channelrhodopsin). Gating is a change in protein conformation that controls ion flux.
- *Feedback control.* A sensor measures the output, a controller (P/I/D) compares it with a setpoint and drives an actuator. Stability depends on loop gain, phase margin and actuator lag. The Modular Core PID (T6) is a working example.
- *Wardenclyffe history (Grounded).* Built 1901–1902 at Shoreham, NY: a ~187 ft (57 m) tower topped by a hemispherical electrode, an earth connection by a ~120 ft shaft with radial pipes, and funding from J. P. Morgan. It never operated commercially; funding ended about 1906, and the tower was demolished in 1917. Its design came from the Colorado Springs magnifying transmitter (1899) [to verify exact dimensions].
- *Tesla's wireless-power claims.* Global transmission of usable power through the Earth and atmosphere was **never demonstrated and is unsupported at scale**. Near-field resonant coupling works only over short ranges, and losses dominate at planetary scale (#86, [geodesic_organic_design_review.md](geodesic_organic_design_review.md)).
