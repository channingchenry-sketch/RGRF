## 1. Microtubules + anesthesia (breakthrough section)

### 1.1 Findings

1. **Khan S. et al., "Microtubule-Stabilizer Epothilone B Delays Anesthetic-Induced Unconsciousness in Rats," *eNeuro* 11(8), ENEURO.0291-24.2024 (2024).** Sources: [eNeuro](https://www.eneuro.org/content/11/8/ENEURO.0291-24.2024), [PMC11363512](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11363512/), PubMed 39147581.
   - Rats were given 0.75 mg/kg epothilone B, a brain-penetrant microtubule stabilizer.
   - They took on average **69 s longer** to lose the righting reflex under 4 % isoflurane. Statistics: N = 8, p = 0.0016, Cohen's d = 1.9.
   - The authors say this is "predicted by models that posit consciousness as a property of a quantum physical state of neural MTs."
   - **The measurement itself is Grounded (one study).**
2. **Babcock N. S. et al., "Ultraviolet Superradiance from Mega-Networks of Tryptophan in Biological Architectures," *J. Phys. Chem. B* 128(17), 4035–4046 (2024).** Sources: [PMC11075083](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11075083/), PubMed 38641327, arXiv 2302.01469.
   - Theory predicts collective (superradiant) UV states across more than 10⁵ tryptophans in microtubule architectures.
   - Experiments showed the **fluorescence quantum yield rises** in larger tubulin assemblies, and the effect persists with disorder at thermal equilibrium.
   - **Grounded:** a quantum-optical effect in a warm biological structure. The excitation is **UV**, and this is not shown to be part of neural signaling.
3. **Kalra A. P. et al., "Electronic Energy Migration in Microtubules," *ACS Cent. Sci.* 9(3), 352 (2023), doi:[10.1021/acscentsci.2c01114](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.2c01114).** Also at [PMC10037452](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10037452/).
   - Tryptophan excitation diffuses about **6.6 nm**, more than conventional Förster theory explains.
   - 50 µM etomidate or isoflurane reduced this to 5.6 and 5.8 nm. Docking suggests weak, non-specific binding to tubulin.
   - **Grounded (in vitro).**

### 1.2 Critiques and limits

- **Decoherence:** M. Tegmark, "Importance of quantum decoherence in brain processes," *Phys. Rev. E* 61, 4194 (2000), doi:[10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194). He estimates decoherence times of about 10⁻¹³–10⁻²⁰ s, against neural timescales of about 10⁻³–10⁻¹ s, and concludes the brain is effectively classical.
- **Rebuttal:** Hagan, Hameroff & Tuszynski, arXiv [quant-ph/0005025](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005025). They recalculate decoherence at about 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ s. **Status: still disputed.**
- **What Khan 2024 does not show:**
  - It does not show *quantum* involvement. Microtubule stabilization could change anesthetic sensitivity through classical routes such as protein trafficking, synaptic structure or drug distribution. The authors' own background notes several other anesthetic targets (ion channels, receptors, mitochondria).
  - The sample was small (N = 8, males only), and it is a single lab's result. It needs replication.
  - Mainstream anesthesia pharmacology still centres on receptors and ion channels such as GABA_A. A primary source for this was not cited here.
- **Babcock and Kalra** show quantum or collective optical effects in microtubules. They **do not show** that these effects carry information for consciousness.
- **Labels:**
  - Microtubules as one functional anesthetic target: **Plausible-untested → weakly supported**.
  - Consciousness as a quantum state of microtubules (Orch-OR): **Speculative**.
  - Microtubules as an "exchange mechanism" between people or with Earth: **Speculative / unsupported**.

## 2. Mitochondria + light (breakthrough section)

1. **Photobiomodulation (PBM): Grounded as a phenomenon, mechanism contested.**
   - M. R. Hamblin, "Mechanisms and Mitochondrial Redox Signaling in Photobiomodulation," *Photochem. Photobiol.* (2018), doi:[10.1111/php.12864](https://doi.org/10.1111/php.12864). It names **cytochrome c oxidase (CCO)** as the main red/NIR chromophore. In the proposed mechanism, light displaces inhibitory nitric oxide, which restores electron transport, membrane potential and ATP.
   - **Against a CCO-only mechanism:**
     - "Photobiomodulation enhancement of cell proliferation at 660 nm does not require cytochrome c oxidase" (2019), [PubMed 30927704](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30927704/). Cells with no assembled CCO still responded. Authors not checked.
     - "Effect of Red-to-Near Infrared Light and a Nitric Oxide Donor on the Oxygen Consumption of Isolated Cytochrome c Oxidase" (2021), [PubMed 34115530](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34115530/). There was no 670/830 nm effect on isolated CCO, and light did not relieve the NO inhibition.
     - Review: "What Lies at the Heart of Photobiomodulation: Light, Cytochrome C Oxidase, and Nitric Oxide," doi:[10.1089/photob.2020.4905](https://doi.org/10.1089/photob.2020.4905). It reports no reliable demonstration of a direct light effect on CCO.
   - **2024–2025 results:**
     - "Photobiomodulation on isolated mitochondria at 810 nm: first results on the efficiency of the energy conversion process," *Sci. Rep.* (2024), doi:[10.1038/s41598-024-61740-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-61740-w). Isolated bovine mitochondria responded to 810 nm light with measurable energy-conversion efficiency, and reciprocity did not hold: the dose alone does not set the effect.
     - Trajano et al., "Does photobiomodulation alter mitochondrial dynamics?" *Photochem. Photobiol.* (2025), doi:[10.1111/php.13963](https://doi.org/10.1111/php.13963). This review covers fusion and fission effects (MFN2, DRP1).
   - **Net:** mitochondria respond to red/NIR light (**Grounded**). The chromophore is **not settled**; CCO, other respiratory complexes, interfacial water and other candidates are proposed.
2. **Ultraweak photon emission ("biophotons"): Grounded, as a by-product of metabolism.**
   - Salari V. et al., "Imaging Ultraweak Photon Emission from Living and Dead Mice and from Plants under Stress," *J. Phys. Chem. Lett.* 16(17), 4354–4362 (2025), doi:[10.1021/acs.jpclett.4c03546](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpclett.4c03546). Live mice emitted much more ultraweak light than the same mice just after death, both held at 37 °C. Stressed plants emitted more.
   - The emission is usually attributed to reactive-oxygen-species chemistry, with mitochondria as a major ROS source.
   - Emission as a **signal used for communication**: **Speculative**.
3. **RGRF link:** light → mitochondria → ATP/ROS is a real lever for the living habitat: cabin lighting, crew health, and fungal photoresponses (fungal light response not verified here). **Plausible-untested** as a design element. Tying it to an "informed universe" is **Symbolic / Speculative**.

## 3e. Dog olfaction, stress chemosignals & aromatic compounds

> Channing (verbatim): "Yes, I was coming at it from a point of an empath. Also, we looked at dogs and there ability to sense through smell we touched on benzene rings and aromatic hydrocarbons."

- **Dogs smell human stress: Grounded (single study).**
  - C. Wilson, K. Campbell, Z. Petzel & C. Reeve, "Dogs can discriminate between human baseline and psychological stress condition odours," *PLOS ONE* 17(9), e0274143 (2022), doi:[10.1371/journal.pone.0274143](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0274143). The work is from Queen's University Belfast; the authors are from the [QUB release](https://www.qub.ac.uk/News/Allnews/2022/Stress-has-an-odour-and-dogs-can-smell-it.html).
  - Four trained dogs picked each person's post-stress breath and sweat sample over that person's baseline sample (taken 4 minutes earlier), with **93.75 % accuracy** over 720 trials (chance = 33 %).
  - This means acute stress changes breath and sweat volatile organic compounds (VOCs). The specific compounds were not identified.
- **Dogs detect disease VOCs: Grounded, but methods vary.**
  - Systematic reviews:
    - Bauër et al., "Remote Medical Scent Detection of Cancer and Infectious Diseases With Dogs and Rats: A Systematic Review," *Integr. Cancer Ther.* (2022), doi:[10.1177/15347354221140516](https://doi.org/10.1177/15347354221140516). The journal was read from the URL prefix.
    - "Canine olfactory detection of SARS-CoV-2-infected humans—a systematic review," [PMC10195768](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10195768/). Higher-quality studies show high sensitivity and specificity, but many studies carry a risk of bias. It also gives the basics: canine olfaction is estimated at **10,000–100,000×** human sensitivity, with detection limits around **parts per trillion**. These are estimates quoted in the review.
  - "Canine olfactory detection and its relevance to medical detection," *BMC Infect. Dis.* (2021), doi:[10.1186/s12879-021-06523-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12879-021-06523-8). For many diseases, the exact molecules dogs respond to are **unknown**.
- **Aromatic (benzene-ring) compounds in stress and disease VOCs: Grounded (specific studies).**
  - **Psychological stress, breath:** "The effect of a paced auditory serial addition test (PASAT) intervention on the profile of volatile organic compounds in human breath: a pilot study," *J. Breath Res.* 7(1), 017102 (2013), doi:[10.1088/1752-7155/7/1/017102](https://doi.org/10.1088/1752-7155/7/1/017102).
    - Six stress-sensitive compounds were found. Three of the confirmed ones are **aromatic**: **indole** (an aromatic heterocycle), **2-hydroxy-1-phenylethanone**, and **benzaldehyde**.
    - Per the abstract, indole *increased* after stress while benzaldehyde and the others *decreased*, possibly from faster breathing. A trade-press summary says the reverse; the abstract is used here.
  - **Lung cancer, breath:** "Target Analysis of Volatile Organic Compounds in Exhaled Breath for Lung Cancer Discrimination…" ([PMC7464039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7464039/)). Strong associations were found for **toluene, ethylbenzene and styrene**, which are monoaromatic hydrocarbons. The authors call them largely **exogenous** (ambient air, smoking), which is an important confound.
  - **COVID-19:** a canine–GC-MS comparison (*ERJ Open Res.* 10(3), 00007-2024, [link](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erjor/10/3/00007-2024)) reported candidate COVID VOCs. Benzaldehyde was listed in a search summary, but that was **not confirmed from the paper text**.

- **Human fear chemosignals: Grounded.**
  - J. H. B. de Groot, M. A. M. Smeets, A. Kaldewaij, M. J. A. Duijndam & G. R. Semin, "Chemosignals Communicate Human Emotions," *Psychol. Sci.* 23(11), 1417–1424 (2012), doi:[10.1177/0956797612445317](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612445317).
  - Fear sweat made receivers show a fearful expression and sniff more; disgust sweat produced disgust and sensory rejection. Receivers were not consciously aware of this.

  - It covered **both** the molecules dogs detect **and** aromatic π-electron and light interactions.

## 3h. Regeneration: deer antlers & hair cycling

> Channing (verbatim): "We went into hair growth and deer antler regeneration as a sort of mechanism that may utilize this concept"

"This concept" refers to the information / coherent-field / bioelectric-patterning thread of this doc: information organizing matter.

1. **Deer antler regeneration: Grounded.**
   - Antlers are the only mammalian organ that fully regenerates every year. They grow up to **~2.75 cm/day** and reach ~15 kg and 120 cm in about 3 months. Source: D. Wang & T. Landete-Castillejos, "Stem cells drive antler regeneration," *Science* 379, 757–758 (2023), doi:[10.1126/science.adg9968](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg9968). Red deer grow about 1.7 cm/day (reviewed in [PMC6822923](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6822923/)).
   - Wang Y. et al., "Genetic basis of ruminant headgear and rapid antler regeneration," *Science* 364, eaav6335 (2019), doi:[10.1126/science.aav6335](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aav6335).
     - Antler gene expression resembles **osteosarcoma** more than normal bone.
     - Proto-oncogenes (FOS, REL, FAM83A) and **p53-pathway tumour-suppressor genes** show positive selection in deer. This fits fast growth with low cancer incidence.
     - Many antler-expressed genes are neural (axon guidance), and the headgear appears to have a neural-crest origin.
     - Volume and first name are from secondary sources.
   - T. Qin et al., "A population of stem cells with strong regenerative potential discovered in deer antlers," *Science* 379, 840–847 (2023), doi:[10.1126/science.add0488](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.add0488).
     - Single-cell atlas: PRRX1⁺ mesenchymal cells give rise to **antler blastema progenitor cells (ABPCs)**.
     - Similar cells exist in the regenerating mouse digit tip.
     - Transplanted ABPCs formed antler-like bone and cartilage on mouse heads.
   - **Pedicle periosteum** as the antler stem-cell source, and the rapid growth of nerves in the velvet: reviewed in "Deer antlers: the fastest growing tissue with least cancer occurrence," [PMC10733395](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733395/). Authors not checked; a specific nerve-growth rate was **not verified** here.
2. **Hair-follicle cycling: Grounded, and a real field-like coordinated pattern.**
   - Follicles cycle through anagen (growth), catagen (regression) and telogen (rest). Bulge stem cells reactivate each cycle (textbook).
   - M. V. Plikus, J. A. Mayer, D. de la Cruz, R. E. Baker, P. K. Maini, R. Maxson & C.-M. Chuong, "Cyclic dermal BMP signalling regulates stem cell activation during hair regeneration," *Nature* 451, 340–344 (2008), doi:[10.1038/nature06457](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06457).
     - Hair regeneration spreads in **waves across the skin**. A dermal BMP rhythm runs out of phase with WNT, which splits telogen into refractory and competent phases.
     - Grafted skin and host skin influence each other's cycling.
     - It is a coordinated, spatially propagating pattern, but its medium is **chemical signalling (BMP/WNT), not an EM field**.
3. **Bioelectric patterning of regeneration: Plausible, supported in model organisms.** This is the grounded bridge to "fields as information."
   - Review: "Bioelectric signaling in regeneration: Mechanisms of ionic controls of growth and form," [PubMed 29291972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29291972/) (Levin lab; authors not checked).
     - A 24-hour ionophore cocktail triggered **hindlimb regrowth in non-regenerating *Xenopus* froglets**.

     - "Bioelectric signaling regulates head and organ size during planarian regeneration," *Development* 140(2), 313 (2013), [link](https://journals.biologists.com/dev/article/140/2/313/76645/). H,K-ATPase-dependent voltage sets head and organ scale.

## 3i. Frisson: aesthetic chills, goosebumps & being moved (general research)

This section is general research only. Frisson means the chills, goosebumps or piloerection people feel at intense, "resonant" moments.

1. **Reward circuitry: Grounded.**
   - A. J. Blood & R. J. Zatorre, "Intensely pleasurable responses to music correlate with activity in brain regions implicated in reward and emotion," *PNAS* 98(20), 11818–11823 (2001), [PMID 11573015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11573015/).

     - [¹¹C]raclopride PET showed **dopamine release in the striatum** at peak emotion.
     - The **caudate** was more involved during **anticipation**, the **nucleus accumbens** during the **peak** itself.
2. **Individual differences: Grounded.**
   - M. E. Sachs, R. J. Ellis, G. Schlaug & P. Loui, "Brain connectivity reflects human aesthetic responses to music," *Soc. Cogn. Affect. Neurosci.* 11(6), 884–891 (2016), [PMID 26966157](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26966157/). People who get chills had greater white-matter volume (DTI tracts) linking the auditory pSTG to the anterior insula and mPFC, which handle emotional and social processing. The sample was small.
   - R. R. McCrae, "Aesthetic Chills as a Universal Marker of Openness to Experience," *Motiv. Emot.* 31, 5–11 (2007), doi:[10.1007/s11031-007-9053-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-007-9053-1).      - In 100 students, frisson frequency (self-report plus skin conductance) correlated with Openness and with 5 of its 6 facets.
     - The **cognitive** facets (attentive, predictive listening) mattered more than had been assumed.
3. **Being moved / kama muta: Grounded.** J. H. Zickfeld et al., "Kama muta: Conceptualizing and measuring the experience often labelled being moved across 19 nations and 15 languages," *Emotion* 19(3), 402–424 (2019), doi:[10.1037/emo0000450](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000450).
   - Study size: 3,542 participants.
   - Trigger: a **sudden intensification of communal sharing**.

4. **Social and intellectual chills: Grounded, with an early evidence base.**

   - Chills during insight and understanding: F. Schoeller & L. Perlovsky, "Aesthetic Chills: Knowledge-Acquisition, Meaning-Making, and Aesthetic Emotions," *Front. Psychol.* 7, 1093 (2016), PMID 27540366. This is a **theory paper**, cited in Bannister 2019 but not read in full here, so treat it as **Plausible**.

   - Sympathetic nerves contract the arrector pili muscle (APM), which raises the hair.
   - Y. Shwartz et al. (Y.-C. Hsu lab, Harvard), "Cell Types Promoting Goosebumps Form a Niche to Regulate Hair Follicle Stem Cells," *Cell* 182(3), 578–593 (2020), [PMC7540726](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7540726/).

## 3j. Empathy, sympathetic reactivity & mirror neurons (general research)

### Empathy and autonomic / sympathetic reactivity

1. **Physiological linkage: Grounded, small n.** R. W. Levenson & A. M. Ruef, "Empathy: a physiological substrate," *J. Pers. Soc. Psychol.* 63(2), 234–246 (1992), doi:[10.1037/0022-3514.63.2.234](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.63.2.234).
   - Design: 31 observers continuously rated how a spouse in a recorded marital interaction was feeling.
   - **Negative-emotion accuracy was highest when the observer's autonomic signals tracked the target's over time.**
   - Positive-emotion accuracy instead went with **low** cardiovascular arousal in the observer, not with linkage.
   - So accuracy tracked synchrony, not simply more arousal.
2. **Interpersonal autonomic physiology: Grounded, with a fragmented literature.** R. V. Palumbo et al., "Interpersonal Autonomic Physiology: A Systematic Review of the Literature," *Pers. Soc. Psychol. Rev.* 21(2), 99–141 (2017), doi:[10.1177/1088868316628405](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868316628405).
   - Physiological synchrony appears across many relationship types and correlates with psychosocial constructs.
   - It flags inconsistent terms, measures and statistics, which makes results hard to compare.
3. **Vicarious skin conductance to others' pain: Grounded.** G. Hein, C. Lamm, C. Brodbeck & T. Singer, "Skin conductance response to the pain of others predicts later costly helping," *PLoS ONE* 6(8), e22759 (2011), doi:[10.1371/journal.pone.0022759](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0022759).
   - Stronger vicarious skin-conductance responses predicted **costly helping** later.
   - So did a closer match between the response to others' pain and to one's own pain.
4. **Shared neural representation of pain: Grounded.**
   - T. Singer et al., "Empathy for pain involves the affective but not sensory components of pain," *Science* 303, 1157–1161 (2004), doi:[10.1126/science.1093535](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1093535).
     - Watching a partner in pain activated the anterior insula and ACC (the affective part of pain), not the sensory pain areas.
     - The activation **scaled with questionnaire empathy scores**.
   - C. Lamm, J. Decety & T. Singer, "Meta-analytic evidence for common and distinct neural networks associated with directly experienced pain and empathy for pain," *NeuroImage* 54(3), 2492–2502 (2011), doi:[10.1016/j.neuroimage.2010.10.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2010.10.014). It confirms an anterior insula and ACC overlap between felt pain and empathy for pain.
5. **Highly sensitive persons (sensory-processing sensitivity, SPS): Grounded as a trait measure, small imaging n.**
   - E. N. Aron & A. Aron, "Sensory-processing sensitivity and its relation to introversion and emotionality," *J. Pers. Soc. Psychol.* 73, 345–368 (1997). It introduced the HSP scale, which correlates with emotionality (r ≈ .46–.58) but is not identical to it.
   - B. P. Acevedo et al., "The highly sensitive brain: an fMRI study of sensory processing sensitivity and response to others' emotions," *Brain Behav.* 4, 580–594 (2014), doi:[10.1002/brb3.242](https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.242).
     - Sample: **18 participants**.
     - Higher HSP scores went with more activation in insula, IFG, cingulate and premotor areas when viewing emotional faces, especially partners and happy faces.
     - That is brain activation, **not a sympathetic-nerve measure**.
6. **Chills link:** in Bannister 2019 (§3i), trait empathy predicted **moving chills** specifically, not warm or cold chills. Chills are a sympathetic (piloerection) marker.
7. **Does "much higher in empathic people" hold? Partly, and not as "much higher."**
   - What holds:
     - Empathy-related autonomic and neural responses to others' pain and emotion are real and measurable.
     - They scale with empathy scores in some studies (Singer 2004 insula and ACC).
     - They predict empathic accuracy (Levenson & Ruef, via **synchrony**) and helping (Hein 2011).
   - What does not:
     - These studies did not show that empathic people have broadly or dramatically higher **sympathetic arousal**.
     - Levenson & Ruef tied positive-emotion accuracy to *lower* arousal.
     - The literature is small-sample and methodologically fragmented (Palumbo 2017).
     - No meta-analytic effect size linking trait empathy to sympathetic reactivity was found or verified here.
   - **Label:** "stronger / more synchronized vicarious responses in empathic people" is **Plausible-supported**; "much higher sympathetic activity" is **not supported as stated**.

### Mirror neurons

1. **Discovery: Grounded.** G. di Pellegrino, L. Fadiga, L. Fogassi, V. Gallese & G. Rizzolatti, "Understanding motor events: a neurophysiological study," *Exp. Brain Res.* 91, 176–180 (1992), doi:[10.1007/BF00230027](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00230027). Neurons in macaque premotor area F5 fired both when the monkey performed an action and when it watched one.
2. **Human single neurons: Grounded.** R. Mukamel, A. D. Ekstrom, J. Kaplan, M. Iacoboni & I. Fried, "Single-neuron responses in humans during execution and observation of actions," *Curr. Biol.* 20, 750–756 (2010), [PMID 20381353](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20381353).
   - Recordings: 1,177 cells in 21 epilepsy patients.
   - Mirror-like cells were found in the SMA and the hippocampus and surrounding areas.
   - Some were "anti-mirror": excited when acting, inhibited when watching. That may help tell self from other.
3. **Broad claims (empathy, language, autism; e.g., Iacoboni): Speculative / contested.**
   - G. Hickok, "Eight problems for the mirror neuron theory of action understanding in monkeys and humans," *J. Cogn. Neurosci.* 21(7), 1229–1243 (2009), [PMC2773693](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2773693/).
     - No monkey data directly test the action-understanding claim.
     - Human dissociations count against it.
   - Hickok expanded this in *The Myth of Mirror Neurons* (Norton, 2014): mirror responses may be learned sensorimotor associations.
   - **"Broken mirror" theory of autism: weak evidence.** A. F. de C. Hamilton, "Reflecting on the mirror neuron system in autism: a systematic review of current theories," *Dev. Cogn. Neurosci.* (2013), [PMC6987721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987721/). It found little evidence of a global mirror-system deficit and argued the theory should be abandoned.
4. **Current view:**
   - Mirror and shared-representation systems exist: **Grounded**.
   - They contribute partly to action perception and imitation: **Plausible-supported**.
   - Their role in empathy is **modest and indirect**. Shared-affect networks such as the anterior insula and ACC carry more of the empathy evidence than motor mirror cells: **Plausible**.

     - Isolated rat fascia contracted with TGF-β1, U46619 and mepyramine.
     - Contraction scaled with myofibroblast density (r_s = 0.83).
     - Forces build over **minutes to hours**, too weak for joint stability but possibly enough to affect motor coordination. Over days to months they can drive contractures (Schleip et al., *Clin. Anat.* 32, 891–895, 2019).
   - The authors note the **sympathetic system can raise TGF-β1 expression**. That makes a sympathetic → TGF-β1 → myofibroblast-tone pathway **Plausible-untested** in living people.
3. **Fascia as an interoceptive organ: Plausible, a review- and book-level claim.**
   - R. Schleip & H. Jäger, "Interoception: a new correlate for intricate connections between fascial receptors, emotion, and self recognition," in *Fascia: The Tensional Network of the Human Body* (Churchill Livingstone, 2012), ch. 2.3.
     - Fascial free nerve endings project to the **insula**, the same hub as the empathy-for-pain network (Singer 2004).
     - A claim that fascia has ~7× more interoceptive than proprioceptive endings is quoted second-hand. It is **unverified**.
   - B. Bordoni & F. Marelli, "Emotions in Motion: Myofascial Interoception," *Complement. Med. Res.* 24, 110–113 (2017). Not read; it is cited in secondary sources.
4. **Interoception and empathy: Plausible, mixed results.**
   - Positive findings:
     - L. Grynberg & O. Pollatos, "Perceiving one's body shapes empathy," *Physiol. Behav.* (2015), [PMID 25497886](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25497886/). With n = 93, better heartbeat perception went with higher pain estimates, compassion and arousal when viewing others in pain.
     - H. Fukushima, Y. Terasawa & S. Umeda, *Int. J. Psychophysiol.* 79, 259–265 (2011), doi:[10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2010.10.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2010.10.015). With n = 21, heartbeat-evoked brain potentials rose during empathy judgments and correlated with empathic concern.
   - Null finding: "Heartfelt empathy? No association between interoceptive awareness, questionnaire measures of empathy, reading the mind in the eyes task or the director task," *Front. Psychol.* (2015), [PMC4416382](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4416382/). Authors not checked.
   - These studies measure **cardiac** interoception, not fascial.
5. **Myofascial work and HRV / vagal tone: Plausible, low-quality evidence.**
   - M. Arroyo-Morales et al., "Effects of myofascial release after high-intensity exercise: a randomized clinical trial," *J. Manipulative Physiol. Ther.* 31(3), 217–223 (2008), doi:[10.1016/j.jmpt.2008.02.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmpt.2008.02.009). In n = 62, 40 minutes of myofascial release brought HRV (including HF power) and diastolic blood pressure back to baseline better than a sham.
   - Systematic review, "Effects of spinal manipulation and myofascial techniques on heart rate variability" (2018), [PMID 29332747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29332747/).
     - 10 studies, 5 of them RCTs.
     - Cervical and lumbar work leaned **parasympathetic**; thoracic manipulation leaned sympathetic.
     - It calls for better methods.
   - A single session of full-body foam rolling did **not** raise HRV ([PMC9683995](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9683995/)).
   - Touch and pressure receptors and expectation could explain the effects without any fascia-specific mechanism.
6. **Chronic sympathetic tone → fascial stiffness: Plausible-untested.** The mechanism is items 1 and 2. No in-vivo human study linking stress to measured fascial stiffness was verified here.
7. **"Emotional release" during bodywork: anecdotal.** It is widely reported by practitioners. No controlled evidence was verified, and touch, safety and attention cues are an ordinary explanation.

**Answer: is there evidence linking fascia to empathy or sympathetic responses?**
- **Sympathetic: yes, partly.**
  - Fascia carries sympathetic fibres: Grounded.
  - Its contractile cells respond to signalling molecules that sympathetic activity can modulate: Grounded in vitro, Plausible in vivo.
  - Myofascial work shows small, low-quality HRV effects.
- **Empathy: no direct evidence.** The only bridge is indirect: fascial afferents feed the insula, the interoception hub that is part of the empathy-for-pain network, and interoception-empathy correlations are themselves mixed. **Fascia → empathy: Speculative.**

## 3k. Epigenetics (general research)

> Channing (verbatim): "The next path we walked down was on epigenetic"

**Focus of the original discussion:** Channing: "we turned to nature". The thread moved to epigenetics in nature: predator–prey effects across generations, then a maze-learning study (§3l).

Links: §3c (stress / allostatic load, GR resistance), §3j (fascia and sympathetic responses), §3g (intent and meditation).

1. **Early care and stress marks on the glucocorticoid receptor (GR): Grounded.**
   - I. C. G. Weaver et al. (Meaney and Szyf labs), "Epigenetic programming by maternal behavior," *Nat. Neurosci.* 7, 847–854 (2004), doi:[10.1038/nn1276](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1276).
     - In rats, high maternal licking and grooming lowered methylation at the hippocampal GR exon 1₇ promoter, raised GR expression and dampened HPA stress responses.
     - Cross-fostering **reversed** the difference, and an HDAC inhibitor erased it in adults. The marks are potentially reversible.
   - P. O. McGowan et al., "Epigenetic regulation of the glucocorticoid receptor in human brain associates with childhood abuse," *Nat. Neurosci.* 12, 342–348 (2009), doi:[10.1038/nn.2270](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2270).
     - Post-mortem hippocampus from suicide victims with childhood abuse showed **more NR3C1 promoter methylation and less GR mRNA** than suicide victims without abuse or controls.
     - This is a small, correlational, post-mortem study.
   - T. Klengel et al., "Allele-specific FKBP5 DNA demethylation mediates gene–childhood trauma interactions," *Nat. Neurosci.* 16, 33–41 (2013), doi:[10.1038/nn.3275](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3275).
     - Childhood trauma caused **demethylation at FKBP5 intron-7 glucocorticoid response elements**, but only in carriers of the rs1360780 risk allele.
     - That raised stress-dependent FKBP5 expression and dysregulated the stress-hormone system: a gene × environment mechanism.
     - FKBP5 dampens GR signalling, so this ties directly to the GR-resistance thread in §3c.
2. **Intergenerational and transgenerational effects: Plausible-contested in humans.**
   - B. G. Dias & K. J. Ressler, "Parental olfactory experience influences behavior and neural structure in subsequent generations," *Nat. Neurosci.* 17, 89–96 (2014), doi:[10.1038/nn.3594](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3594).
     - Male mice fear-conditioned to acetophenone had F1 and F2 offspring more sensitive to that odour, with larger Olfr151 glomeruli and sperm Olfr151 hypomethylation.
     - IVF and cross-fostering argued for transmission through the gametes.
     - **Critique:** G. Francis, "Too Much Success for Recent Groundbreaking Epigenetic Experiments," *Genetics* 198(2), 449 (2014), [link](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/198/2/449/5935942). Given the sample sizes, the uniform success was statistically improbable. Dias & Ressler replied that they had replicated it in their own lab ([PMC4196603](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4196603/)). Independent replication was not verified here.
     - The acetophenone link: this is the odorant from the vibrational-olfaction critique in §3e.
   - Dutch Hunger Winter: B. T. Heijmans et al., "Persistent epigenetic differences associated with prenatal exposure to famine in humans," *PNAS* 105(44), 17046–17049 (2008), doi:[10.1073/pnas.0806560105](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0806560105).
     - People exposed to famine around conception had about **5.2% lower IGF2 methylation** roughly 60 years later. Late-gestation exposure had no effect.
     - This is **in-utero** exposure, so intergenerational, not transgenerational. Volume, pages and DOI were written from memory; the key result was confirmed in a search summary.
   - R. Yehuda et al., "Holocaust Exposure Induced Intergenerational Effects on FKBP5 Methylation," *Biol. Psychiatry* 80(5), 372–380 (2016), doi:[10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.08.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.08.005).
     - Sample: 32 survivors, 22 offspring, 8 parent and 9 offspring controls.
     - Survivors had higher, and offspring lower, methylation at FKBP5 intron 7 bin 3/site 6.
     - **Critiques:** a tiny sample, limited controls, and no way to separate germline transmission from in-utero, parenting or shared-environment effects. See "Transgenerational epigenetic inheritance: a critical perspective," *Front. Epigenet. Epigenom.* (2024), doi:[10.3389/freae.2024.1434253](https://doi.org/10.3389/freae.2024.1434253).
   - **Label:** inheritance through the germline is shown in some animal models but contested. In humans, **transgenerational** inheritance (beyond direct in-utero exposure) is **not established**.
3. **Meditation and epigenetics: Plausible, small studies.**
   - P. Kaliman et al., "Rapid changes in histone deacetylases and inflammatory gene expression in expert meditators," *Psychoneuroendocrinology* 40, 96–107 (2014), [PMC4039194](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4039194/).
     - Design: 19 experienced meditators against 21 leisure controls, over one 8-hour day.
     - Meditators showed lower **HDAC2/3/9** expression, histone-mark changes, and lower **RIPK2 and COX2** (pro-inflammatory genes).
     - Lower HDAC2 and RIPK2 went with faster cortisol recovery after the Trier Social Stress Test.
     - It was not randomized, and controls had no meditation experience.
   - R. Chaix et al., "Differential DNA methylation in experienced meditators after an intensive day of mindfulness-based practice," *Brain Behav. Immun.* 84, 36–44 (2020), doi:[10.1016/j.bbi.2019.11.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2019.11.003). It found 61 differentially methylated sites, enriched in immune and inflammation pathways.
   - The **epigenetic clock** study is Chaix et al., *Psychoneuroendocrinology* 85, 210–214 (**2017**), doi:[10.1016/j.psyneuen.2017.08.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psyneuen.2017.08.016). The 2020 paper is the methylation-day study above.
     - Average epigenetic age acceleration was **similar** in meditators and controls.
     - Older controls showed acceleration that older meditators did not.
     - Acceleration fell with years of practice.
     - Cross-sectional; the authors call for RCTs.
4. **Epigenetic clocks: Grounded as a biomarker.** S. Horvath, "DNA methylation age of human tissues and cell types," *Genome Biol.* 14, R115 (2013), doi:[10.1186/gb-2013-14-10-r115](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2013-14-10-r115). It uses methylation at 353 CpG sites to estimate age across tissues. Citation details were written from memory and are well known; the Chaix 2017 abstract confirms the method.
5. **Labels:**
   - Mechanisms (DNA methylation, histone modification, GR/FKBP5 stress programming): **Grounded**.
   - Reversibility in adulthood: **Grounded in rats**, Plausible in humans.
   - Human transgenerational inheritance: **Plausible-contested**.
   - Meditation shifting epigenetic marks: **Plausible**, small, mostly non-randomized studies.
   - "Intent rewriting the map" (§3g) through epigenetics: **Speculative**. The meditation data are a suggestive hook, not proof.
   - Epigenetic marks stored in **fascia** (§3j): **Speculative**, no study found. Fascial fibroblasts have epigenomes like any cell, but no study links them to emotion.

### Predator–prey: fear carried into the next generation

1. **Daphnia inducible defences across generations: Grounded.** A. A. Agrawal, C. Laforsch & R. Tollrian, "Transgenerational induction of defences in animals and plants," *Nature* 401, 60–63 (1999), doi:[10.1038/43425](https://doi.org/10.1038/43425).
   - Water fleas (*Daphnia cucullata*) exposed to *Chaoborus* predator **kairomones** grew larger helmets.
   - Their offspring had larger helmets **even without exposure**, and the effect reached the grandchildren.
   - Wild radish showed the same pattern for herbivore damage.
   - This is a chemical cue acting through the mother: a maternally induced defence.
2. **Snowshoe hare, predator stress → offspring: Grounded.**
   - M. J. Sheriff, C. J. Krebs & R. Boonstra, "The sensitive hare: sublethal effects of predator stress on reproduction in snowshoe hares," *J. Anim. Ecol.* 78, 1249–1258 (2009), doi:[10.1111/j.1365-2656.2009.01552.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2656.2009.01552.x). Pregnant hares exposed to a dog had offspring **37% lighter and 16% smaller**, and maternal cortisol metabolites predicted the decline.
   - Sheriff, Krebs & Boonstra, "The ghosts of predators past: population cycles and the role of maternal programming under fluctuating predation risk," *Ecology* 91, 2983–2994 (2010), doi:[10.1890/09-1108.1](https://doi.org/10.1890/09-1108.1); volume and pages written from memory.
     - Maternal stress hormones tracked predator density and were echoed in the offspring, whose HPA axes were more responsive.
     - This is offered as a reason hare populations stay low after predators decline.
     - Mechanism: stress hormones passed from mother to young (maternal programming), the same GR pathway as §3k.
3. **Landscape of fear: Grounded.** L. Y. Zanette, A. F. White, M. C. Allen & M. Clinchy, "Perceived Predation Risk Reduces the Number of Offspring Songbirds Produce per Year," *Science* 334, 1398–1401 (2011), doi:[10.1126/science.1210908](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1210908).
   - Design: real predation was blocked with electric fencing and nets, and predator calls were played back to song sparrows.
   - **Perceived risk alone cut offspring by 40%**: fewer eggs, lower hatching and more nestling deaths.
4. **Predator-odour stress across generations in rodents:** this was not separately verified here. The verified rodent cross-generation odour result is Dias & Ressler 2014 (§3k), which used a conditioned odour, not a predator odour. It remains contested.
5. **Label:** predator-induced **intergenerational** effects through maternal hormones or cues are **Grounded** in Daphnia, hares and birds.

### The "maze across distance" study: most likely the McDougall rat experiments

**[Channing to confirm]** whether this is the study.

The animals were **rats, not mice**. The "distance" element is Sheldrake's later reinterpretation, not the original design.

1. **William McDougall (Harvard, from 1920), Lamarckian test: Grounded as historical data.**
   - W. McDougall, "An experiment for the testing of the hypothesis of Lamarck," *Br. J. Psychol.* 17, 267 (1927), followed by later reports (1930, 1933 with J. B. Rhine, 1938).
   - Rats learned to escape a water tank by choosing the dim exit and avoiding a lit, shocked gangway.
   - Over **32+ generations**, trained lines made progressively **fewer errors**.
   - McDougall had **no properly run control line**, as noted by Agar et al.
2. **F. A. E. Crew (Edinburgh), replication: Grounded.** "A repetition of McDougall's Lamarckian experiment," *J. Genet.* 33, 61 (1936), doi:[10.1007/BF03027604](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03027604).
   - It ran 18 generations, with more rats per generation and trained plus control lines.
   - It found **no progressive improvement in either line**.
   - Crew argued that generation averages depend on the mix of fast and slow rats, which breeding choices can shift.
3. **W. E. Agar, F. H. Drummond, O. W. Tiegs & M. M. Gunson (Melbourne), 20-year replication: Grounded.** "Fourth (Final) Report on a Test of McDougall's Lamarckian Experiment on the Training of Rats," *J. Exp. Biol.* 31(3), 307–321 (1954), doi:[10.1242/jeb.31.3.307](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.31.3.307).
   - Interim reports appeared in 1935, 1942 and 1948.
   - They **confirmed long-run improvement trends**, but the effect was **not sustained**.
   - It was **shown equally by control rats of untrained ancestry**, which "forbids a Lamarckian interpretation."
   - Their analysis tied the trends to the rats' **condition and the colony's health**, which went through periods of decline and recovery.
4. **Sheldrake's reading: Speculative.** R. Sheldrake, *A New Science of Life* (1981; details from memory) cited the improvement **in the untrained control lines** as possible **morphic resonance**: learning spreading to unrelated rats, even in a different country.
   - That is the "learning across distance" Channing recalls.
   - It is **Speculative**. There is no controlled, independent, pre-registered confirmation, and the original authors explained the trends with colony health and selection.
   - Crew's null result is hard to square with a field effect.
5. **Labels:**
   - The McDougall, Crew and Agar observations: **Grounded** historical data.
   - The Lamarckian interpretation: **refuted** by the controls.
   - Morphic resonance: **Speculative**.
   - Any link to collective consciousness: **Speculative / Symbolic**.
