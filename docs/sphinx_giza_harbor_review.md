## 3. Water erosion and the age debate [2][6][8]

- **Mainstream.** Carved in the 4th Dynasty. The enclosure weathering comes from late Old Kingdom rains plus salt weathering, wind and pre-existing joints in layered limestone. **Grounded** as the consensus position.
- **Contested.** Robert Schoch's precipitation-erosion argument, presented to the GSA in 1991 and published in *KMT* 3(2) in 1992 [to verify page], dates the core to ~7000–5000 BCE or earlier. Colin Reader's quarry-runoff view argues for an Early Dynastic, somewhat earlier date. Both are minority positions. They are not fringe in the sense of lacking data, but most Egyptologists and geologists reject the older dates.
- **Seismic survey [8].** Dobecki & Schoch's early-1990s refraction work in the enclosure, finding a weathered zone that deepens toward the front, is **Grounded** as a survey. Its age interpretation is **Contested**. Grok's figures, "~1.8–2.5 m" depth and "groundwater ~14.5–15.5 m elevation" (AERA), are [to verify].
- **Channing's view.** "I’m sure this was eroded by weather and water" [5] matches the Schoch side. The data show water was involved in the weathering; the dispute is over *when*, so the claim should be labelled **Contested**, not settled.
- **LiDAR and remote sensing [6].** Grok says no confirmed hidden chambers under the Sphinx, and that SAR "Doppler tomography" pyramid claims (2022–25) are unverified. **Grounded:** that is the current state. The 2024 Western Cemetery GPR/ERT anomaly is real but preliminary ([to verify] citation).

## 4. Harbour and waterways [8][10]

- **"Sphinx as a dock" [7].** **Speculative**, but not unreasonable as a question.
  - The Sphinx and valley temples sat at the edge of a harbour and canal system. Sheisha et al., *PNAS* 119(37), e2202530119 (2022), doi:10.1073/pnas.2202530119, show that the Khufu Branch stayed high during the 4th Dynasty.
  - Valley temples functioned as river harbours (Ghoneim et al., *Commun. Earth Environ.* 5, 233 (2024), doi:10.1038/s43247-024-01379-7, the Ahramat Branch).
  - There is no evidence the Sphinx itself was a quay.
- **Khufu harbour / "She Khufu" and Merer's diary [10].** **Grounded.** The Wadi el-Jarf papyri, published by Tallet, record Tura limestone deliveries to "She Khufu." Grok's harbour extent of "~400 × 475 m" is [to verify].

## 5. Alignments, π circles, 60°, golden rectangle [12]–[22]

- **Cardinal alignment.** **Grounded.** The Great Pyramid's sides are ~3–4 arc-minutes from true north (Petrie; Cole 1925). **Correction:** the "<37–45 arc seconds" in [26] is too tight for orientation [to verify]. It may confuse orientation with the squareness of the base corners.
- **π in the Great Pyramid [16].** The arithmetic is **Grounded**: 4b/(2πh) = 1.0002 (rerun). It follows from the 5½-palm seked, since perimeter/height = 8 × 11/14 = 44/7 = 2 × 22/7. Whether π was *intended* is **Contested**; most specialists see it as a side-effect of the seked. "This isn't accidental — surveys confirm the precision" overstates it, because precision does not show intent.
- **"Pi expansion" circles from pyramid centres [15][16][20][22].** **Symbolic.** Any set of points can be overlaid with circles, so overlap with temples or causeways is not evidence without a pre-registered test. The "~2,384 m radius through Kafr el-Gebel" arc [14] comes from alternative-research layouts [to verify source]: **Speculative**.
- **60° arcs [18].** **Symbolic.** 60° comes from hexagon and equilateral geometry. Grok cites no measured 60° relation at Giza.
- **Golden rectangle [18].** **Correction / Symbolic.** Grok itself gives length/height ≈ 3.65. The rerun gives L/H ≈ 3.64, L/W ≈ 3.85 and H/W ≈ 1.06, none near φ = 1.618. "Selective proportions sometimes approximate phi" is cherry-picking, so it is **Symbolic** unless tested.
- **Ley lines [11][12].** Watkins' alignment history is **Grounded**. "Energy" leys and Giza as a "global node" are **Symbolic**.

## 6. "Charging ports … early Wardenclyffe-pyramids" [23][24][26]

- **Speculative.** There is no archaeological or physical evidence of electrical function. The **Mainstream** reading is tombs with mortuary temples. The **Grounded** part of Channing's options is "really accurate celestial markers": stellar alignment methods for cardinal orientation are well studied (e.g., Spence, *Nature* 408, 320 (2000)) [to verify].
