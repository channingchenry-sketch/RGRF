## 7. Mayim Bialik, Prader-Willi syndrome, CRISPR (messages 18–22)

- **Bialik:** B.S. (2000) and Ph.D. (2007) in neuroscience from UCLA. Her dissertation was on hypothalamic regulation of obsessive-compulsive, affiliative and satiety behaviours in Prader-Willi syndrome. **Grounded.** Grok noted the spelling ("Bialek" in the prompt).
- **PWS mechanisms:** loss of paternally expressed genes at 15q11.2–q13 (SNRPN, the SNORD116 cluster, MAGEL2). Causes are paternal deletion (~65–75%), maternal uniparental disomy (~20–30%), and imprinting defects (a few percent). Incidence is about 1 in 10,000–30,000. **Grounded.** Diazoxide choline (VYKAT XR) was FDA-approved in March 2025 for hyperphagia in PWS. **Grounded.** Oxytocin and carbetocin results are mixed, as Grok said. **Grounded.**
- **CRISPR-Cas9:** off-target cuts, large deletions and mosaicism are documented risks, and high-fidelity Cas9, base editors and prime editors reduce them. The 2018 He Jiankui germline-edited babies led to his conviction and to the call for a moratorium on heritable editing. Somatic therapy is approved (Casgevy, 2023). **Grounded.** Germline editing remains ethically contested and is not clinically permitted in most jurisdictions.
- Grok's links from PWS and CRISPR back to POL v0.1 and the docking nodes ("bio-inspired tech") are **Symbolic**.
