## Concepts as described (earlier note)

1. **Regenerative Housing Community Plan.** A "phi-spiral modular layout using bamboo frames, mycelium panels, and hempcrete walls, with shared systems and living architecture." Images: `images/Regenerative_Housing_Community_Plan.jpg`.
2. **Mycobam Modular Bio-Composite Block System.** "Interlocking blocks combining bamboo reinforcement lattice, mycelium structural core (Trametes versicolor), and hempcrete thermal shell, with phi-proportioned connectors." Images: `images/Mycobam_Interlocking_Bio_Composite_Blocks.jpg`.
3. **Mycelium Bubble Homes.** "Temporary transit shelters: rapidly deployable, grown mycelium shells on hemp/bamboo frames." Images: `images/Temporary_Transit_Mycelium_Bubble_Homes.jpg`.

The note itself says all three are "conceptual (CC0)". It gives no dimensions, loads, R-values, assembly details or test data.

## Labelled assessment

| Element | Label | Reality check |
|---|---|---|
| Hempcrete walls | Grounded (as infill/insulation) | Hempcrete is **non-load-bearing**. It is cast or sprayed around a structural frame (usually timber). Typical density is ~300–500 kg/m³; compressive strength is only on the order of 0.1–1 MPa; conductivity is roughly 0.06–0.1 W/m·K. It is good for insulation, humidity buffering and carbon storage, and cures slowly (weeks to months). In the US it is covered by IRC Appendix BL (hemp-lime construction, from the 2024 IRC). Local adoption varies. |
| Bamboo frame | Grounded, with conditions | Structural bamboo is real (ISO 22156 design standard; ISO 22157 testing). It must be preservative-treated against beetles and fungi; **borax/boric-acid treatment** is the common low-toxicity method. It must also be kept dry and off the ground. Its joints are the weak point. In many jurisdictions engineered bamboo or an engineer's stamp is needed for permits. |
| Mycelium panels | Grounded as insulation / non-structural panels; Plausible-untested as structure | Mycelium composites are light (~100–500 kg/m³ for most published materials), with compressive strength in the ~0.1–3 MPa range depending on substrate and pressing. They are water-sensitive and need a protective skin, and must be heat-killed (inactivated) before use. Commercial use today is packaging, acoustic and insulation panels. There are no building-code approvals for load-bearing use that I know of. |
| Mycobam interlocking blocks (bamboo lattice + mycelium core + hempcrete shell) | Plausible-untested | Each ingredient exists, but the combination is untested. Open questions: bond between layers, moisture movement, creep under load, fire behaviour, and how blocks transfer load if the hempcrete and mycelium carry little. The bamboo lattice would have to do the structural work. |
| Phi-proportioned connectors / phi-spiral layout | Speculative (aesthetic/design heuristic) | Fine as an architectural motif. There is no known structural or thermal advantage to φ proportions. |
| Mycelium bubble homes (temporary) | Plausible-untested | Grown mycelium shells (e.g. art pavilions such as the Hy-Fi tower, 2014) show short-term feasibility. As shelter, the unsolved points are weather resistance, durability, fire, ventilation/condensation and growth time (days to weeks). |
| Airform (inflatable-form) domes (mentioned, not specified) | Grounded | Monolithic domes are built by inflating an airform, spraying polyurethane foam inside, then rebar and shotcrete. They are proven, very wind and fire resistant, and engineered per project. They are not inherently regenerative (foam and cement carry high embodied carbon). A regenerative variant would swap materials, and that variant is untested. |
| "Shared systems and living architecture" | Plausible-untested / undefined | No systems are specified (water, energy, waste). |
| "Net-positive" | Speculative until quantified | It needs a life-cycle assessment (embodied carbon vs. sequestered carbon in hemp, bamboo and mycelium). |

## Gaps to fill before this is a spec
- Dimensions, floor area and storeys; structural system and loads (dead, live, snow, wind, seismic).
- Wall build-ups with thickness, U-value/R-value and a moisture strategy (vapour-open finishes such as lime plaster for hempcrete).
- Fire rating, and test data for blocks and panels.
- Foundation type. Bamboo and mycelium must stay off the ground with a capillary break.
- Code path (IRC Appendix BL, ISO 22156, or alternative-materials approval).
- Climate zone and site.
- The new dome/housing spec, and a list of exactly how it differs from this earlier note.

## Bench / pilot tests suggested
1. **Hempcrete wall panel on a timber or bamboo frame.** Cure it and log moisture. Measure density and thermal conductivity with a heat-flux meter.
2. **Mycobam block coupons.** Run compression and creep tests (wet vs. dry), plus a simple flame-spread comparison.
3. **Small grown-mycelium shell.** Run a 3–6 month outdoor exposure test with and without a lime or natural-oil skin.
