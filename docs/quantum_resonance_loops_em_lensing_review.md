## 1. EM lensing and halo claims ([43]–[46])
| Claim | Label | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Plasma lensing: refraction by electron-density gradients, frequency-dependent, strongest at radio; seen in FRBs and pulsars | Grounded | Refractive index n ≈ 1 − ν_p²/2ν². The bending angle scales as ν⁻², so it is chromatic. Seen in extreme scattering events, pulsar scintillation and FRB 20180916B-type lensing |
| Plasma lenses could explain "anomalous" cosmic lensing | **Not supported** | Galaxy and cluster lensing is **achromatic** from radio to X-ray. Plasma lensing at optical wavelengths is ~10⁶–10⁸ times weaker than at GHz. Plasma cannot replace dark-matter lensing |
| Lensing "smooths" our view of the halo, so the halo looks constant from inside | Partly corrected | Lensing does not average away the halo. The Milky Way's halo is measured with stellar and gas rotation curves, Gaia kinematics and microlensing. Microlensing *rules out* most compact halo objects (MACHO/EROS/OGLE: < ~10–20 % of the halo in 10⁻⁷–10 M_☉ objects) |
| Sgr A*'s well "masks" halo variations; inner halo core ~200–300 pc | Speculative / Grok-reported | Sgr A* (4×10⁶ M_☉) dominates only within a few pc. Whether the inner halo is cored or cuspy is debated [to verify the 200–300 pc figure] |
| NFW profile ρ ∝ 1/r inner, r⁻³ outer; core–cusp and missing-satellite tensions; fuzzy and self-interacting DM alternatives | Grounded | |
| Dark energy as "increasing pressure density" | **Corrected** | Dark energy acts through *negative* pressure (w ≈ −1, so p ≈ −ρc²). Positive, rising pressure would *decelerate* expansion |
| Dark matter as metallic-hydrogen/helium clumps | **Not supported** | (a) BBN and the CMB fix baryons at Ω_b ≈ 0.049 against Ω_m ≈ 0.31, so ~84 % of matter must be non-baryonic. (b) Microlensing excludes compact baryonic halos. (c) Metallic H needs ~400–500 GPa and is not known to survive at zero pressure. (d) Baryonic clumps would emit and absorb; the Bullet Cluster separates gas from lensing mass. Grok's caveat was too soft ("without contradicting data outright"), because it does contradict the data |
| Birkeland currents (aurora ~10⁶ A), filamentation, z-pinch | Grounded | |
| Plasma-cosmology galaxies from filament pairs with flat rotation curves (Peratt) | Speculative / not supported | Does not match CMB acoustic peaks or large-scale structure |

## 2. Axion, neutrino and dark-matter claims ([55]–[56])
| Claim | Label | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Axions solve strong CP (Peccei–Quinn), are cold DM via misalignment, searched by ADMX and CAST, not yet detected | Grounded | |
| μeV axion mass ↔ "de Broglie wavelengths on galactic scales" | **Corrected** | λ = h/(m v). At m = 10 µeV and v = 220 km/s, λ ≈ **170 m**, not galactic. Galactic (kpc) wavelengths need m ~ 10⁻²² eV ("fuzzy" DM), a different regime |
| Axions "behave like a classical field oscillating coherently" | Grounded | Occupation numbers are huge. Oscillation frequency ν = m c²/h ≈ 2.4 GHz for 10 µeV, which is what ADMX tunes to |
| Neutrino oscillations from mass and mixing; confirmed by solar, atmospheric, reactor and accelerator experiments; MSW matter effect; collective oscillations in supernovae | Grounded | |
| Oscillations "don't directly probe dark matter" | Grounded | Relic neutrinos are hot DM, capped at Σm_ν ≲ 0.1–0.2 eV, under ~1 % of DM. A sterile neutrino is a separate candidate |
| Plasma neutrino production in stars and supernovae | Grounded | Also the plasmon-decay process γ* → νν̄, which cools white dwarfs and red-giant cores |
| **Rotating neutron stars beam neutrinos like a lighthouse** | **Corrected** | Pulsars beam *electromagnetic* radiation (radio to γ). Neutrinos come out almost isotropically, mainly in the ~10 s birth burst (SN 1987A) and early cooling. No pulsed neutrino emission is expected or seen |
| Axion–neutrino couplings exist "in some models" | Speculative | Model-dependent; no evidence |
