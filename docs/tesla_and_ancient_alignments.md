## A. Tesla
*See also:* [tesla_davinci_threads.md](tesla_davinci_threads.md) (Tesla and Da Vinci threads, the Vitruvian Man, charge balance and expansion).

### Documented history (Grounded)
- **Colorado Springs, 1899.** Tesla built a high-voltage, high-frequency laboratory and ran large resonant-transformer ("magnifying transmitter") experiments, producing very long artificial sparks. The lab was later razed (1904) to settle debts.
- **Wardenclyffe, Shoreham NY, 1901–1917.** Construction began in 1901, and the ~187 ft tower was completed around 1902. Financing (J. P. Morgan) ended, and the project was never completed or operated as intended. The tower was demolished for salvage in 1917.

### Claims vs. evidence
| Claim | Label | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tesla demonstrated global wireless power transmission | Not demonstrated | No documented measurement of useful power received at a distance from Colorado Springs or Wardenclyffe. Wardenclyffe never became operational. |
| Earth–ionosphere cavity resonance exists | Grounded | Schumann resonances: fundamental ~7.8 Hz (often quoted as 7.83 Hz), higher modes ~14, 20, 26, 33 Hz. They are excited mainly by lightning. |
| Use the Schumann cavity to distribute power | Speculative / impractical | The cavity is lossy (fundamental Q ≈ 4–5). The band is extremely low-frequency and very narrow, so it carries little power. The wavelength (~40,000 km) needs planetary-scale antennas for efficient coupling, and the energy spreads over the whole globe. |
| Resonant near-field wireless power | Grounded | Kurs et al., "Wireless Power Transfer via Strongly Coupled Magnetic Resonances", *Science* 317, 83–86 (2007): 60 W at ~40 % efficiency over >2 m with self-resonant coils. This is the basis of WiTricity, Qi chargers and EV pads. It is a near-field technique effective over a few coil diameters, not global. |

**Falsifiable test for "Tesla-style" global power claims.** Build a transmitter of stated power and frequency and place a calibrated receiver at distance d. Compare the measured received power with the standard near-field, ground-wave and radiative models. Any repeatable excess with the transmitter switched on (blind, with switching controls) would be significant.

## B. Ancient civilisations and stone alignments

### Archaeoastronomy (Grounded)
- **Newgrange (Ireland, c. 3200 BCE):** a "roof-box" above the entrance lets winter-solstice sunrise light travel down the passage into the chamber for about 17 minutes.
- **Stonehenge (England):** the principal axis aligns with summer-solstice sunrise (near the Heel Stone) and, in the opposite direction, winter-solstice sunset.
- **Chichén Itzá, El Castillo (Mexico):** around the equinoxes, afternoon light makes a serpent-like shadow appear on the northern stairway's balustrade. The effect lasts several days around each equinox.
- These show deliberate solar observation and calendar-keeping, which is well established.

### Ley lines
*See also:* Channing's "cosmic filaments (vast ley lines)" note in [em_topo L5b](em_topological_gravity_review.md#l5b-filament-riding-casimir-resonance-chamber--quartz-tuning-grok-excerpt-recovered-2026-10-15) (ley lines there: Symbolic).

| Claim | Label | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Watkins' leys as ancient straight trackways / sightlines | Plausible-untested (historical hypothesis) | Alfred Watkins presented the idea to the Woolhope Club in Sept 1921 and published *Early British Trackways* (1922) and *The Old Straight Track* (1925). He proposed sighting lines between mounds, stones, churches, etc., for travel. He did **not** propose energy lines. |
| Ley lines as "energy lines" (earth energy, telluric/magnetic grids) | Speculative | This came later (1960s–70s New Age writers). No measured physical field follows claimed leys. |
| Alignments are more frequent than chance | Not supported in tested cases | Broadbent, "Simulating the Ley Hunter", *J. R. Stat. Soc. A* 143, 109–140 (1980), showed suggested Cornish leys were consistent with random site distribution. Kendall & Kendall, "Alignments in two-dimensional random sets of points", *Adv. Appl. Prob.* 12, 380–424 (1980), developed formal tests. With many sites, chance near-alignments are common. |

**Falsifiable tests.**
1. **Field survey.** Walk calibrated magnetometer (and optionally VLF/radio-noise) transects across claimed ley lines and across matched control lines (same terrain and geology, randomly placed). Record blind, and analyse whether claimed lines show anomalies at a higher rate than controls.
2. **Statistical alignment test.** For a regional site list, compare observed near-collinear triads/tetrads with Monte-Carlo random sites drawn from the same density map (the Broadbent / Kendall method). Pre-register the acceptance angle.
3. **Astronomical test** (for a claimed astronomical alignment): compute the horizon azimuth of solstice/equinox sunrise or sunset for the site's epoch, including horizon altitude, and compare it with the structure's axis and uncertainty.
