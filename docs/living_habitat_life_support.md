## Checks
### 1. Living structural habitat
- **NASA Mycotecture Off Planet: Grounded** (verified).
  - **Lead:** Lynn Rothschild, NASA Ames.
  - **Phases:** NIAC Phase I in 2018, Phase II in 2021, and **Phase III in 2024**: $2 M over two years, toward low-Earth-orbit testing and a possible lunar demo.
  - **Sources:**
    - NASA, "NASA Advances Research to Grow Habitats in Space from Fungi" (news release, 2024): https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-advances-research-to-grow-habitats-in-space-from-fungi/
    - NIAC project page: https://www.nasa.gov/general/mycotecture-off-planet/
    - Phase II final report: NTRS 20250001059.
  - The "dormant fungi + lightweight frame + add water" description matches NASA's text.
- **"Kept partially alive for ongoing repair":** NASA's concept **bakes the mycelium to kill it** once grown, for structure and planetary protection. Self-healing only applies to living mycelium, so cured composites have limited or no self-repair. The paste's "self-healing hyphal networks" under "Proven" is overstated: **Plausible-untested** for living skins.
- **Trametes versicolor** is a white-rot fungus and is **not typically melanized**. Melanin-based shielding would need a different or engineered strain; NASA reports incorporating melanin-producing strains into experiments.
- **Radiation claim, overstated.** The flight data: *Cladosporium sphaerospermum* (a melanized fungus) grown on the ISS gave **~2.17 ± 0.25 % lower** radiation counts beneath a ~1.7 mm lawn than the control, extrapolated to ~4.3 % for full enclosure.
  - Source (verified): N. J. H. Averesch et al., "Cultivation of the dematiaceous fungus *Cladosporium sphaerospermum* aboard the International Space Station and effects of ionizing radiation," *Front. Microbiol.* 13, 877625 (2022), doi:10.3389/fmicb.2022.877625 (preprint bioRxiv 2020.07.16.205534).
  - "Outperforming equivalent regolith" appears to be a **per-thickness modelling extrapolation** in that work, not a measured head-to-head result, and the authors also note alternative explanations. **Flag:** overstated as "NASA data". The study was not a NASA mycotecture result.

### Checks
- **Co–MnO nanocubes on mycelium-derived carbon: found and verified.**
  - Source: X. Li, Z. Yang, S. Li, M. Gao & Y. Liu, "Interfacial engineering of Co–MnO nanocubes on mycelium carbon enabling synergistic bifunctional water splitting," *Int. J. Hydrogen Energy* (2026), doi:10.1016/j.ijhydene.2026.153716.
  - The carbon is **carbonized *Ganoderma lucidum* (reishi) mycelium**, N-doped, made by pyrolysis. It is **not living mycelium** and not Trametes.
  - Reported in **alkaline** electrolyte: HER overpotential 105 mV at 10 mA/cm², OER 250 mV at 50 mA/cm², and a full cell at 1.61 V at 50 mA/cm², with good durability.
  - Lab-scale only: **Grounded** as a published lab result; craft use **Plausible-untested**; radiation, long-duration and variable-gravity performance **Speculative** (as the paste says).
- **ISS Oxygen Generation Assembly: Grounded** (verified, NASA ICES papers on NTRS).
  - It uses a **PEM-type, cathode-feed electrolysis cell stack**.
  - Feedwater first passes an **Inlet Deionizing Bed**, which removes iodine and coalesces gas bubbles. This is **deionization before the stack**: Grounded.
  - A **Rotary Separator Accumulator (RSA)** separates product H₂ from the recirculating water: **rotary separation: Grounded**.
  - H₂ goes to the Sabatier or is vented.
  - Sources:
    - "International Space Station United States Orbital Segment Oxygen Generation System On-orbit Operational Experience" (NTRS 20080036243).
    - ICES-2023-311 (NTRS 20230008013).
- **Silver ion biocide: Grounded.**
  - The **Russian segment** (and Mir before it) uses **silver** as the potable-water biocide, while the **US segment uses iodine**.
  - NASA is developing **silver electrolysis** to replace iodine dosing in future spacecraft potable water, targeting ~200–400 ppb Ag.
  - Sources: "Preliminary Testing of Electrolytic Silver Ion Generation…" ICES-2020 (NTRS 20205003735); "Spacecraft Potable Water: 2024 Update," ICES-2024-045 (NTRS 20240004872).
- **Sabatier: Grounded.** CO₂ + 4H₂ → CH₄ + 2H₂O. The ISS Sabatier CO₂ Reduction Assembly was installed in 2010; it recovers water from OGA hydrogen and cabin CO₂. Microfluidic CO₂ electrolyzers are a research area: **Plausible-untested**.
- **Feed from the mycelium loop:** the inlet water quality from a living bioremediation loop is unknown. Organics and microbes foul PEM membranes, so conditioning (DI bed + biocide) is essential: **Plausible-untested** as specified.
- **Tesla-resonant power for the stack: Plausible-untested.** Near-field only (coil-scale distances), and electrolyzers need low-voltage, high-current DC, so a rectifier/converter is required regardless.
- **Phi-spiral O₂ ducting: Speculative** (no φ-specific benefit shown).
- **Hazard ties (§10f):** H₂ flammability (~4–75 % in air), H₂ + O₂ separation, H₂ permeation, and **embrittlement of ferritic steel** back-iron. See [helium_water_circulation.md §10f](helium_water_circulation.md).

### Sodium acetate (recalled; details [Channing to supply])
> Channing (verbatim): "Right, we ran into that before and started looking into ionic bonding I believe Na acetate"

Context: membrane fouling from mycelium-loop feedwater. The **"ionic bonding" role is [Channing to clarify]**.
- **Grounded properties:**
  - **Buffer:** acetate/acetic acid is a buffer pair (pKa ≈ 4.76).
  - **Less corrosive than chloride brines:** no chloride pitting of steels.
  - **Biodegradable.**
  - **Deicer:** sodium and potassium acetate are used as low-corrosion deicers, e.g. on airport runways. Widely documented, but not separately verified here.
  - **Phase-change heat storage:** sodium acetate **trihydrate** melts at ~58 °C with latent heat ~250–265 kJ/kg, and it **supercools** (the click-to-start hand warmer). That makes it a candidate **warm-side** thermal store, complementing the ~0 °C water/ice buffer of [helium_water_circulation.md §9](helium_water_circulation.md).
- **Caveats:**
  - **Acetate is a carbon source.** It **feeds microbes**, which could worsen biofouling, or could be used deliberately to feed the mycelium or consortia.
  - **It does not fix PEM fouling.** PEM stacks need deionized water, and **Na⁺ poisons PEM membranes** by exchanging onto the sulfonic sites and lowering conductivity. Sodium acetate is better suited to **alkaline electrolyzers** or the **brine/electrolyte loop** ([helium_water_circulation.md §10](helium_water_circulation.md)).
- **Confirmed use (Channing, verbatim):** "Yeah we used it as a feed". Sodium acetate was used **deliberately as a carbon feed for the mycelium**.
  - Many fungi can use acetate as a carbon source (Grounded).
  - Dosing must be controlled and confined to the mycelium zone so it doesn't feed unwanted bacteria or cause biofouling elsewhere in the loops: **Plausible-untested**.
- **Status:** Plausible-untested.
- **Delivery by copper (Channing, verbatim):** "Yeah we used copper as a delivery method I think it also served multipurpose". Copper tubing delivered the acetate feed. Its other roles were multipurpose (recalled): **[Channing to supply]**.
  - **Grounded:**
    - High thermal conductivity, ~400 W/m·K, so the feed line doubles as a heat path.

    - Could double as a coil or conductor.
    - Related prior use: copper tubes run through the shell for airflow and mould deterrence in the borax-cure notes ([open_grow_kit.md](open_grow_kit.md#grow-in-place-then-cure-borax--copper-ventilation)). Also the copper half-tube control in [butterfly_node.md](butterfly_node.md).

## Living vs non-living zones; petrified tubes as designed branches
> Channing (verbatim): "We zoned living vs non living zones, looked into petrification for tubes as well like a designed branch but again build up and deposits could become an issue"

1. **Living vs non-living zoning (recalled design fact).**
   - Keeping **fungitoxic or biocidal materials** (copper, silver dosing, terpenes, B₄C/BN-treated hot sinks) in **non-living zones** and the fed mycelium in **living zones** resolves the earlier conflicts (copper ions, terpenes and biocides vs living mycelium).
   - It matches the selective approach elsewhere: cured, inert composite where structure or heat matters, living layers only where repair or bioremediation is wanted. See the [mycelium envelope zone](pol_v0_1_zones.md#4-mycelium-hemp-structural-envelope) and the baked-shell + living-layer logic of NASA mycotecture (above), plus [mycelium_uses.md](mycelium_uses.md).
   - Zone interfaces need barriers (liners, check valves, a separate return). **Plausible-untested.**
2. **Petrified / mineralized tubes as designed branches.**
   - **Grounded analogs:**
     - **Microbially induced calcite precipitation (MICP)**, e.g. *Sporosarcina pasteurii* urease, which precipitates CaCO₃. See the MICP thread in [coral_wave_barriers.md](coral_wave_barriers.md) and [open_grow_kit.md](open_grow_kit.md).
     - **Fungal biomineralization** (fungi precipitate calcium carbonates and oxalates).
     - **Silica petrification** (mineral replacement of organic structure).
     - Grown channel networks (mycelium or scaffold templates mineralized into tubes).
   - **Caveat (Channing's):** continued **scaling and deposits narrow the bore** over time, as in pipe scaling and calcified catheters.
   - **Mitigation ideas (Plausible-untested):**
     - **stop mineralization once formed** (a cure/kill step, or remove the feed for the mineralizing organism);
     - a **periodic mild-acid / acetate flush** (acetic acid and acidic acetate solutions weakly dissolve calcite; ties to the acetate feed);
     - a **smooth or hydrophobic inner liner** (BN/PP, §10c of [helium_water_circulation.md](helium_water_circulation.md));
     - keep the **flow velocity above the scaling/settling threshold**, so wall shear limits deposit growth.
   - Also note MICP releases **ammonium** by-product, which needs managing in a closed loop.

## Cabin vibration for bone density ("cat purr influenced")

  - E. von Muggenthaler, "The felid purr: A healing mechanism?" *J. Acoust. Soc. Am.* 110(5), 2666 (2001). This is a **conference abstract** (ASA meeting), not a peer-reviewed paper; flagged as such.
  - The idea that purr frequencies aid healing is a **popular claim, Plausible-untested**.
- **Low-magnitude, high-frequency (LMHF) vibration: Grounded research.** ~0.3 g at ~20–90 Hz has been studied for bone.
  - C. Rubin et al., "Low mechanical signals strengthen long bones," *Nature* 412, 603–604 (2001), doi:10.1038/35088122 (sheep, 30 Hz, 0.3 g).
  - **Human trials show mixed results.** It has been proposed as a **microgravity countermeasure** for spaceflight bone loss.
- **Delivery:** floor or seat vibration platforms in the cabin (whole-body vibration through the feet or seat).
- **Safety:** avoid the **~4–8 Hz whole-body resonance** band (vertical), where vibration discomfort and health risk peak (ISO 2631-1, whole-body vibration evaluation). Keep amplitudes low.
- **Unknowns:** frequency and amplitude chosen, daily dose, platform design, and crew-health monitoring.
