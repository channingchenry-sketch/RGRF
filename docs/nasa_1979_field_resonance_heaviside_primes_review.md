## 1. The real 1979 document (verified on NTRS, 2026-10-25)

The 1979 document is **not** a contractor report by H. D. Froning; the brief's guess was wrong. It is:

- **Alan C. Holt**, NASA Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center, Houston. *Field Resonance Propulsion Concept*.
- **NASA-TM-80961 / JSC-16073**, a Technical Memorandum, **August 1979**. NTRS ID **19800010907**, accession 80N19184, Spacecraft Propulsion and Power category. Public, GOV_PUBLIC_USE_PERMITTED. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19800010907
- The memo had no technical review ("TECHNICAL_REVIEW_TYPE_NONE" in the NTRS metadata).

**What it claimed (Grounded, as a description of the document):**
- The NTRS abstract proposes "a resonance between coherent, pulsed electromagnetic wave forms, and gravitational wave forms (or space-time metrics)". It outlines a spacecraft system "potentially capable of galactic and intergalactic travel", drawing on magnetic field-line merging, hydromagnetic waves, free-electron lasers, laser-generated megagauss fields and special containment metals. It lists the research that would be needed.
- The foreword, which I checked against the NTRS full text, reads verbatim: "The concept was developed as the result of private, unofficial research. NASA is not involved in UFO research." It was presented at the 15th Joint AIAA/SAE/ASME Propulsion Conference (June 18–20, 1979), in the session "Propulsion Concepts for Galactic Spacecraft".
- The memo has a section headed "UFO RESEARCH" that it uses as motivation. Grok's summary of this was fair.

**Status:** a conceptual study. It rests on two assumptions the memo itself calls unproven: spacetime as a projection of a higher-dimensional space, and an EM–gravity coupling. Nothing in it was tested. Grok's answer that it "was not an operational system" is **Grounded**.

This holds for the earlier review too: share #96 ([holt_hau_searl_fput_jitterbug_review.md](holt_hau_searl_fput_jitterbug_review.md)) already identified this memo. #144 goes further, into what followed it and the Heaviside, primes and coils topics.

## 4. Oliver Heaviside (msgs 4–6)

- **Grounded:**
  - He reduced Maxwell's equations to the four-vector form we use today, and developed vector notation and operational calculus.
  - He predicted the Kennelly–Heaviside layer, the distortionless-line condition and energy flow through the field. He reached the energy-flow result independently of Poynting (1884–85).
- **Grounded:** the operational impedance Z(p) = R + Lp + 1/(Cp), the ancestor of Laplace and phasor methods. Grok's distortionless-line condition "RC = LG" is correct; it is equivalent to R/L = G/C.
- **Symbolic:** the alignment with RGRF is methodological (practical, field-centred thinking), not a physical claim.
- The earlier Heaviside mention is in [gravitoelectromagnetism_revisit.md](gravitoelectromagnetism_revisit.md) (Heaviside's 1893 gravitational analogy).

## 6. Coils by structure and element, and the "resonant periodic table" (msg 7)

- **Grounded:** coil inductance, mutual coupling and Q depend on geometry (the path to the focus) and on the material's σ and μr, together with skin depth and Curie temperature. Ferromagnets shift resonance and Q. Cu, Ag and Al lose less but store less magnetic energy.
- **Plausible-untested, and actionable:** a materials table (σ, μr, density, Curie point) giving f₀ and Q shifts for a fixed phi-spiral coil [Channing to supply the coil geometry].
- **Speculative, not supported:** that electron configurations "have a resonant match which is the reason for the periodic table". Periodicity comes from quantum numbers, the Pauli principle and Aufbau. Atomic "resonances" are scattering states (Feshbach or shape resonances), not coil frequencies. Grok was right to separate the two.
- **Symbolic:** the RGRF regenerative periodic table and the [helium_octave_table.md](helium_octave_table.md) curiosity, which do not replace the IUPAC table.
