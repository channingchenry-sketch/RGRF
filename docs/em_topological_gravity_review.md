## Context: where Electric Universe (EU) stands
These are respectful but honest points.
- EU / plasma-cosmology claims that EM forces replace or dominate gravity on astronomical scales **conflict with a large body of precise evidence**:
  - planetary ephemerides and spacecraft navigation (Newtonian/GR gravity to high precision);
  - binary-pulsar orbital decay;
  - LIGO/Virgo gravitational-wave detections;
  - gravitational lensing;
  - the cosmic microwave background power spectrum;
  - Big Bang nucleosynthesis abundances.
- Astrophysical bodies are very close to electrically neutral. Spacecraft measure the solar wind as quasi-neutral, and the Sun's net charge is tiny. EM forces between neutral bodies therefore cannot mimic gravity's universal, composition-independent 1/r² attraction.
- The equivalence principle has been tested to about 1 part in 10¹⁵ (the MICROSCOPE mission). Any EM substitute would show composition dependence and fail this.
- Plasma physics itself (filaments, Birkeland currents, double layers) is mainstream and real. Where EU leans on those phenomena it is on solid ground; where it uses them to replace gravity, it is not.

## What a falsifiable test would look like
For any "EM-topological gravity" claim:
1. **Torsion balance or precision weighing of a knotted-field device.**
   - Build a device with high stored magnetic helicity (e.g. a toroidal and poloidal winding pair, or a spheromak-like plasma), with energy E.
   - GR predicts an extra gravitational mass of E/c², so a 1 kJ device gives ~10⁻¹⁴ kg.
   - Any measurable weight change at a 10⁻⁹–10⁻⁶ kg sensitivity, after strict EM, thermal and magnetic-force controls (nulling against identical non-helical windings), would falsify standard physics. A null result falsifies the claim.
   - The prediction must be stated *before* measuring.
2. **Equivalence-principle / composition test.** If gravity is "EM residual", attraction should depend on charge or composition. Existing torsion-balance results (Eöt-Wash) and MICROSCOPE already constrain this to ~10⁻¹³–10⁻¹⁵. A new proposal must predict a deviation larger than these limits somewhere they haven't tested.
3. **Astronomical prediction.** EU models would need to predict quantitatively something GR + ΛCDM gets wrong, for example a specific deviation in planetary perihelia, lensing or the CMB peaks, and then be checked against data.

- Both have measured the very local ISM directly:
  - Interstellar magnetic field ~0.4–0.6 nT at Voyager 1 (average ~0.49 nT); Voyager 2 saw somewhat stronger fields.

  - Gurnett & Kurth, "Plasma densities near and beyond the heliopause from the Voyager 1 and 2 plasma wave instruments", *Nature Astronomy* 3 (2019), doi:10.1038/s41550-019-0918-5.
  - Gurnett et al., "In situ observations of interstellar plasma with Voyager 1", *Science* (2013).
- Order of magnitude: magnetic pressure B²/2μ₀ ≈ 1×10⁻¹³ Pa at 0.49 nT. Thermal pressure of the ionised gas (n ~ 0.05 cm⁻³, T ~ 10⁴ K) is ~10⁻¹⁴ Pa, so the ionised-plasma beta is below 1. Neutral gas and cosmic rays also contribute.

**"Possibly scalar" (Grounded maths).**
- An isotropic rank-2 tensor is necessarily a scalar times the identity, T_ij = p·δ_ij. Physically, that is a pressure.
- So "possibly scalar" is mathematically exact for an isotropic tensor: it reduces to one number.
- Claims about "scalar waves" (Tesla/Bearden-style longitudinal EM waves in vacuum) are a separate idea and **Speculative**: Maxwell's equations in vacuum have no free longitudinal EM wave. Longitudinal *plasma* oscillations (Langmuir waves) are real, and Voyager's density measurements come from exactly those plasma-frequency oscillations.

**Vector equilibrium (geometry; Speculative as physics).**
- Fuller's vector equilibrium is the cuboctahedron: 12 vertices equidistant from the centre, with radial edges equal in length to circumferential edges. It is a genuine geometric balance of radial and circumferential vectors, and the arrangement of nearest neighbours in face-centred-cubic close packing.
- As a picture of an "isotropic" stress state, the analogy is apt: balanced radial and tangential components.
- As a physical mechanism (energy from geometry, a "zero-phase" vacuum state), it is Speculative. No equations connect it to field stresses.

**Maxwell stress tensor (Grounded).**
- T_ij = ε₀(E_iE_j − ½δ_ijE²) + (1/μ₀)(B_iB_j − ½δ_ijB²).
- For a single static magnetic field, this is an isotropic magnetic pressure B²/2μ₀ minus a tension B²/μ₀ along the field lines. A single static field's stress is therefore **never isotropic**. The same holds for a single electrostatic field: tension along E, pressure across it.
- A **randomised/turbulent** field with no preferred direction averages to an isotropic pressure: ⟨B_iB_j⟩ = (⟨B²⟩/3)δ_ij, giving a net isotropic pressure of ⟨B²⟩/6μ₀, like radiation pressure u/3.
- This is relevant to the ISM, which has both a large-scale (ordered) field and a turbulent component. The ordered part gives anisotropic stress; the turbulent part contributes isotropic pressure.

**RGRF extension.** The ISM just outside the heliopause behaves as a near-isotropic, possibly scalar "pressure" medium that balances the heliosphere, read as a vector-equilibrium state. Labelled Speculative; the pressure-balance part is Grounded.

**Falsifiable question.**
- Do Voyager 1/2 magnetometer and plasma-wave data in the very local ISM show the stress state the RGRF version predicts? That means near-isotropic (turbulence-dominated) stress versus ordered-field anisotropy, at a stated level and on stated scales.
- Standard models predict a mostly ordered, draped interstellar field (anisotropic stress) with a smaller turbulent fraction. If RGRF predicts a specific isotropy level, or cuboctahedral (12-fold) structure in field-direction statistics, the Voyager data can test it.

### Casimir checkpoint (current point)
*Channing's recovered notes. They are OCR-damaged: exponents and prefactors below were **reconstructed** and checked against the original papers. Links: Emergent / entropic gravity, R9.*

**1. Casimir plates (Grounded).**
- **Setup:** two parallel perfect conductors a distance d apart allow only the cavity modes k_z = nπ/d between them, while the modes outside remain continuous.
- **Result:** the difference in zero-point energy gives an attractive pressure **P = −π²ħc / (240 d⁴)**, and the energy per area is E/A = −π²ħc/(720 d³).
- **Source:** H. B. G. Casimir, "On the attraction between two perfectly conducting plates," *Proc. Kon. Ned. Akad. Wetensch.* 51, 793–795 (1948).

**2. Casimir–Polder forces (Grounded).**
- These are **potentials** (energies), not forces. For a force, add one power of distance: F = −dU/dr.
- **Atom–wall** (distance z from a perfect conductor):
  - Short range (non-retarded): U ∝ −1/z³ (Lennard-Jones type).
  - Long range (retarded): U = −3ħcα / (8π z⁴).
- **Atom–atom** (separation r):
  - Short range: the London–van der Waals interaction, U ∝ −1/r⁶.
  - Long range (retarded): U = −23ħc α₁α₂ / (4π r⁷).
- The crossover happens at distances around the atomic transition wavelengths.
- **Source:** H. B. G. Casimir & D. Polder, "The influence of retardation on the London–van der Waals forces," *Phys. Rev.* 73, 360–372 (1948), doi 10.1103/PhysRev.73.360.
- Channing's note gave "1/r⁶ → 1/r⁷" for atom–surface. Those are actually the atom–atom forms; the atom–wall forms are 1/z³ → 1/z⁴.

**3. Zeta regularization (Grounded as a technique).**
- ζ(−1) = −1/12 gives a finite value to the divergent sum Σn. In the 1-D (scalar-string) Casimir toy model, it yields E = −πħc/(24 d).
- For the 3-D plates, the relevant value is ζ(−3) = 1/120, which produces the 240 in the pressure formula.
- This is a regularization (equivalent to physical cutoff plus subtraction), **not** a claim that 1 + 2 + 3 + … equals −1/12 in the ordinary sense.

#### 1. Planet-to-space mode switching with trimming coils

- **Trim / shim coils: Grounded.**
  - In magnet systems, small auxiliary coils apply **fine corrections** on top of a main field, cancelling gradients or error fields.
  - The best-documented case is MRI/NMR shimming: nested coils shaped as spherical-harmonic field terms (first to third order) homogenize B₀. See C. Juchem & R. A. de Graaf, "B0 magnetic field homogeneity and shimming for in vivo magnetic resonance spectroscopy," *Anal. Biochem.* 529, 17–29 (2017), doi:[10.1016/j.ab.2016.06.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ab.2016.06.003).
  - Tokamaks and accelerators use similar error-field and trim coils (general knowledge; not separately cited here).
  - Trim coils **shape or correct** a field. They are not a primary source of thrust.
- **What changes between planet and space: Grounded.**
  - *Near a planet (in atmosphere or over a surface):* lift and trim can come from buoyancy (helium), ionic wind / electroaerodynamics (needs air), and EDS/EMS/Halbach interactions with a **conducting surface or track** (needs a reaction body nearby). See lift §1, §4, §9.
  - *In space:* there is no air, so no buoyancy and **no ionic wind**, and no nearby conductor for EDS.
  - What remains is magnetic interaction with **ambient fields and plasma** (magnetorquer torque, magsail / E-sail drag on the solar wind; see L5b), plus conventional reaction propulsion.
  - Ambient fields fall from ~25–65 μT at Earth's surface to tens of nT near the magnetopause (below), so magnetic forces and torques shrink accordingly.
- **The switching concept: Plausible-untested.**
  - Changing actuator mode as the medium changes is ordinary engineering. Aircraft switch control laws, and spacecraft hand off from aero surfaces to thrusters and magnetorquers.
  - Re-tasking the **same coil set** from near-surface trim (EDS/EMS correction, ionic-thruster field shaping) to space use (magnetorquer dipole, or field shaping for a magsail loop) is plausible as a design idea, but untested.

#### 2. Aligning in the magnetosphere

- **Magnetorquers / magnetic attitude control: Grounded.**
  - A coil with dipole moment **m** feels torque **τ = m × B** and turns to align with the local field (a compass needle is the passive version).
  - Small satellites use three orthogonal magnetorquers to detumble and point (NASA SP-8018, "Spacecraft magnetic torques," 1969, [NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19690020961/downloads/19690020961.pdf?attachment=true)).
  - CubeSat-class dipoles are ~0.1–0.5 A·m², giving **micro-newton-metre** torques in low Earth orbit (a search summary of design papers, not opened).
  - Limits: there is **no torque about the B axis** at any instant, so control relies on the field direction changing along the orbit. And the result is **torque, not thrust**.
- **Field strength and fall-off: Grounded.**
  - The surface field is roughly **25,000–65,000 nT (25–65 μT)**: about 30 μT at the equator and 60 μT at the poles ([NOAA NCEI geomagnetism FAQ](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/geomagnetism-frequently-asked-questions); [IGRF notes](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/international-geomagnetic-reference-field/health-warning)).
  - It is close to a tilted dipole, so it falls off roughly as **1/r³**. Approximate dipole values: ~100 nT at geostationary orbit (6.6 R_E), and ~30 nT at 10 R_E. Near the magnetopause the field is compressed, so real values are somewhat higher.
- **Magnetopause: Grounded.**
  - The dayside boundary sits at ~8–11 R_E, where solar-wind pressure balances the geomagnetic field (see the Planetary pulse-confinement cycle in this file).
  - Reconnection (Dungey) and flux transfer events let plasma and energy leak through (R4, C6).
  - Beyond it, the interplanetary field is a few nT.
- **Van Allen belts: Grounded.**
  - Trapped energetic particles. The inner belt is ~1,000–6,000+ km up (NASA describes ~1,000–8,000 miles); the outer belt is ~13,000–60,000 km, varying with solar activity ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/radiation-belts-with-satellites/); [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/probing-the-electric-space-around-earth-78985/)).
  - Practical consequence: **radiation dose and electronics hazard**. Transit fast or shield; do not loiter.
- **Plasma flows: Grounded.**
  - Birkeland currents along auroral field lines (L5).
  - Ring current and plasma sheet.
  - Solar wind outside the magnetopause (~400 km/s, a few protons/cm³; L5b).
- **Labels:**
  - Aligning a craft with Earth's field for **attitude control** via magnetorquers: **Grounded**, flight-proven.
  - Coil field shaping to "align" with magnetospheric structure for navigation sensing: **Plausible-untested**. Magnetometer-based attitude and orbit determination is real; how the RGRF version would work is **[Channing to clarify]**.
  - Using the magnetosphere for **propulsion beyond attitude control**: **Speculative**.
    - Magnetic torque gives no net force in a uniform field.
    - The gradient force on a dipole, F = ∇(m·B), is tiny at these field strengths.
    - Electrodynamic tethers (current × B) are the nearest Grounded exception. They are mainly a de-orbit/drag technology, and the tether physics is not reviewed here.

- **Grounded:**

### R6. Controlled leakage, sheath dynamics & dovetailing by design
- **Grounded:**
  - **Reconnection as an energy entry point:** Dungey (1961, see R4). Reconnection at the dayside magnetopause opens field lines, and solar-wind energy and plasma enter the magnetosphere. The leak is *regulated* by the IMF direction, strongest for a southward IMF.
  - **Plasma sheath physics:**
    - Langmuir, "The interaction of electron and positive ion space charges in cathode sheaths", *Phys. Rev.* 33:954–989 (1929), doi 10.1103/PhysRev.33.954. Sheaths form at every plasma–wall boundary and limit current by space charge.

Ions must enter the sheath at ≥ the ion sound speed (Bohm criterion). So ion flux to a wall ≈ 0.6 n e c_s is set by plasma conditions, while the wall bias sets the electron flux and ion energy.
  - **Gated ion channels as selective leakage:** Hodgkin & Huxley, "A quantitative description of membrane current and its application to conduction and excitation in nerve", *J. Physiol.* 117:500–544 (1952), doi 10.1113/jphysiol.1952.sp004764. Voltage-dependent Na⁺/K⁺ conductances act as boundaries that leak selectively and on command.

This is standard combustion engineering; no single citation was verified, so it is described without one. Links to **C7** (rocket swirl injectors & plume dovetailing; see `C1-C12_seed_map.md`).
  - **Magnetic window:** E×B / static-field depletion of the re-entry plasma layer to open an RF window, a deliberately engineered leak. See `shifting_phase_order_review.md` §B2. Its literature citation is still flagged there as unverified.

  - Design boundaries that leak *selectively*, by species, energy, direction or timing, for exchange rather than sealing them.
  - Interlock ("dovetail") counter-flows through shear layers the way injectors do.
  - This is the engineered counterpart of the natural leaky boundaries in R4–R5.
  - **Plausible-untested** as a unifying design principle. Each component is Grounded on its own.
- **Falsifiable bench question:**

### R7. Cation / charge separation
**Goal:** separate charge (cations from anions or electrons) on purpose, then use the gradient.
- **Grounded:**
  - **Mass spectrometry / calutron:** E. O. Lawrence's calutrons separated ions by mass/charge in a magnetic field. They were used at Oak Ridge **Y-12** for electromagnetic uranium enrichment in the Manhattan Project.
  - **Plasma mass filters:** Ohkawa & Miller, "Band gap ion mass filter", *Phys. Plasmas* 9:5116–5120 (2002), doi 10.1063/1.1523930 (Archimedes Technology Group). Crossed axial B and radial E confine ions below a cutoff mass and lose heavier ions to the wall.
  - **Electrodialysis and ion exchange:** cation- and anion-selective membranes or resins, driven by an applied field or by exchange equilibria, separate salts industrially (desalination, water softening). These are standard processes; no single citation was verified, so they are described without one.
  - **Thunderstorm non-inductive charging:** Takahashi, "Riming electrification as a charge generation mechanism in thunderstorms", *J. Atmos. Sci.* 35:1536–1548 (1978). Graupel–ice-crystal collisions separate ~10⁻⁴ esu (~33 fC) per collision, with sign set by temperature and liquid water content.
  - **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase:** Jens C. Skou, Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1997 (shared), for discovering the ion-transporting enzyme. It pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP, building the membrane gradient that the gated channels in R6 then leak selectively.
  - **Biased sheath as a charge separator:** see R6. A negative wall bias repels electrons and collects ions at the Bohm flux.
- **RGRF extension:** charge separation is the "work" step and selective leakage (R6) is the "use" step. A regenerative device would pair a separator (pump) with a gated leak (channel), like a cell membrane. **Plausible-untested** as a design pattern; Grounded component by component. Any claim that it yields net energy is **Speculative**, because separation always costs at least the free energy of the gradient.
- **Falsifiable bench test:**
  - Glow-discharge or electrolyte cell with two biased segmented electrodes (separator) plus a third gated electrode (leak).
  - Measure collected charge vs input energy.
  - Prediction: separated charge × potential ≤ electrical input, and efficiency is limited by sheath and ohmic losses.
  - If measured stored energy exceeds input, the instrumentation is wrong.
  - The real test is what fraction is recoverable through the gated leak at different gate timings.

**Luneburg lens: a gradient-index analog (Grounded).**

### 2. Sun: plasma structuring, not a crystalline lattice
- **Observed (Grounded).** The Sun is a fully ionised plasma, not a solid or crystalline lattice. Its structure arises from:
  - **Hot/cold interactions:** convection in the outer ~30 % of the radius. Visible as granulation (~1,000 km cells, minutes-long) and supergranulation (~30,000 km cells, ~1–2 days).
  - **Magnetic coupling:** magnetic flux tubes that rise to form sunspots and active regions, and the tachocline, a shear layer at ~0.7 R☉ between the rigidly rotating radiative interior and the differentially rotating convection zone. The tachocline is believed to be central to the solar dynamo and its ~11-year (22-year magnetic) cycle.
  - **Pressure differentials:** the inner-vs-outer pressure and temperature gradient drives radiative transport inside and convection outside. Magnetic pressure (B²/2μ₀) competes with gas pressure in sunspots and the corona.
- **RGRF extrapolation.**
  - That such plasma structuring is a template for regenerative "organised field" design: Plausible-untested as an *inspiration/analogy*.
  - That it implies a crystalline or geometric (φ) order in the Sun: Speculative. Helioseismology shows no crystalline structure.
- **Falsifiable question.** Does any measured solar periodicity or scale (granule sizes, supergranule sizes, rotation-rate ratios, cycle periods) show a statistically significant golden-ratio or other proposed geometric ratio beyond what standard convection/dynamo models predict?

- **Lab analogs (Grounded, citations verified):**
  - Jansson, Haspang, Jensen, Hersen & Bohr, "Polygons on a Rotating Fluid Surface", *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **96**, 174502 (2006). A rotating bottom plate in a stationary cylinder produces rigidly rotating polygonal free surfaces with up to 6 corners. The cause is strong azimuthal shear.
  - Barbosa Aguiar, Read, Wordsworth, Salter & Yamazaki, "A laboratory model of Saturn's North Polar Hexagon", *Icarus* **206**, 755–763 (2010). Barotropically unstable jets/shear layers in a rotating tank equilibrate into polygons, including hexagons. The paper proposes the hexagon is such an equilibrated wave mode of Saturn's jet.
- **RGRF extrapolation.**
  - Hexagon as evidence of geometric resonance "locking" in rotating fluids/plasmas: partly Grounded (shear-instability wavemode selection is real).
  - Hexagon as evidence that geometry (3-6-9 or φ) itself organises the flow: Speculative. The wavenumber is set by jet width, shear and rotation (Rossby number), not by number symbolism.
- **Falsifiable question.** In a rotating-tank experiment, does the polygon number N track the predicted shear/Rossby-number dependence? Or does it prefer 6 (or φ-related ratios) independent of those parameters? The published experiments show N varying with rotation and shear, which supports the fluid-dynamics explanation.

- **Channing's variant: "two flow paths and a resonant bottom" (Plausible-untested).**
  - The published lab analogs drive polygons by *differential rotation* between two flows (a rotating plate vs. stationary walls, or a forced jet/shear layer).
  - The RGRF variant keeps the two flow paths but makes the bottom plate *oscillating/resonant* (vibrated or pulsed), so a drive frequency is added alongside the shear.
  - This is physically reasonable: forced oscillation can lock wave modes (parametric excitation, as in Faraday waves). But it has not been demonstrated for rotating-shear polygons, to my knowledge.
  - This is where the earlier Grok sessions landed: the archived 29 Aug 2026 thread framed the hexagon as a ribbon-Rossby / interior-differential-rotation problem.
- **Falsifiable bench test.**
  - Setup: a rotating tank (rotating bottom plate, stationary wall) with the bottom plate also vibrated vertically by a shaker at a controlled frequency f_d and amplitude.
  - Sweep the shear (rotation rate) at fixed f_d, then sweep f_d at fixed shear. Record polygon mode number N and its rotation rate (overhead camera).
  - **Prediction to test:** if the resonant bottom matters, N (or the polygon rotation rate) locks to f_d over a range of shear where the unvibrated tank would change N. If N tracks only shear/rotation as in the published experiments, the resonant-bottom hypothesis is falsified for this setup.
  - Control runs: no vibration, and vibration at non-commensurate frequencies.

### 4. Jupiter: fuzzy (dilute) core & metallic hydrogen
- **Observed (Grounded, citations verified):**
  - **Dilute core.** Juno's precise gravity harmonics (J2–J8) are best fit by models in which heavy elements are dissolved and spread through roughly 0.3–0.5 of Jupiter's radius. This is a "fuzzy" or dilute core of ~7–25 Earth masses of heavy elements, rather than a compact one.
    - Wahl et al., "Comparing Jupiter interior structure models to Juno gravity measurements and the role of a dilute core", *Geophys. Res. Lett.* 44 (2017), doi:10.1002/2017GL073160.
    - Stevenson, "Jupiter's Interior as Revealed by Juno", *Annu. Rev. Earth Planet. Sci.* 48, 465–489 (2020): "A core exists but is diluted by hydrogen". Winds extend to ~3,000 km depth.
  - **Dynamo in metallic hydrogen.** Under compression, hydrogen becomes a metallic (electrically conducting) fluid. Convection there generates the field. Juno field models put the effective dynamo radius near ~0.8 R_J, at the outer part of the metallic region.
  - **Field maps.** Moore et al., "A complex dynamo inferred from the hemispheric dichotomy of Jupiter's magnetic field", *Nature* 561, 76–78 (2018).
    - Most flux emerges in a narrow northern band, and some returns through an intense, isolated equatorial patch (the "Great Blue Spot").
    - The non-dipolar field is concentrated in the north; the south is mostly dipolar.
    - The authors propose radial layering in density and/or conductivity.
    - Later Juno work attributes most observed secular variation to deep zonal winds shearing the Great Blue Spot.
- **Channing's hypothesis (Speculative).** The core / metallic-hydrogen boundary acts *repulsive or refractive* to the magnetic field, rather than coupling to it.
- **How it relates to real concepts:**
  - **Stably stratified layers:** a compositional gradient (the dilute core) or a helium-rain layer can suppress convection. That would confine or shape where the dynamo operates. This is mainstream and is one of Moore et al.'s suggested explanations. It is the closest grounded cousin of the idea.
  - **Helium rain:** helium immiscibility in metallic hydrogen, which can form a stable layer. This is predicted by theory and supported indirectly (Saturn's helium depletion; Jupiter's atmospheric helium).
  - **Skin-depth screening:** a conducting, stably stratified layer *outside* the dynamo filters time-varying field components (skin effect, δ = √(2/(μ₀σω))). This is real and can smooth or "refract" what is seen outside. A layer *inside* the dynamo region mostly affects the boundary conditions.
  - **Diamagnetism:** genuinely repulsive, but material diamagnetic susceptibilities are ~10⁻⁵. That is negligible next to dynamo fields, so it is not a credible repulsion mechanism at planetary scale.
- **Falsifiable question.** Does a refractive/repulsive boundary predict *different* Juno observables from standard layered-dynamo models? For example:
  - specific flux-concentration patterns (where Great Blue Spot-like patches form);
  - the hemispheric dichotomy;
  - the magnetic power spectrum's apparent source radius;
  - the rate and pattern of secular variation.
  
  If its predictions match standard stratified-layer models exactly, it adds nothing testable. If it predicts something they don't, Juno's extended-mission data can check it.
- **Missing:** a stated mechanism (what physical property makes the boundary repulsive or refractive), the equations (boundary conditions on B and the induction equation), and predicted magnitudes.
