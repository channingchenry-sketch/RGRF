## 3. Myco-, phyco- and UV synthesis of nanoparticles [4]–[10]

- **Grounded.** Fungal (cell-free filtrate) and algal green synthesis of ZnO, Ag and Au nanoparticles is a large literature. The steps Grok lists (precursor salt, biomolecule reduction and capping, UV-Vis/XRD/TEM/FTIR characterisation) are the standard ones. The link cards (Scientific Reports items) are kept as captions only.
- **Correction (mechanism).** For ZnO, the biomolecules mostly *cap* and template the particles; there is no reduction step. Zn²⁺ is not reduced to metal. ZnO forms through hydroxide precipitation and dehydration, usually helped by heat or calcination. Grok's "Biomolecules donate electrons to reduce Zn²⁺ → ZnO" [8] is wrong as stated. Laccase-driven reduction is proven for Ag⁺, as Grok says.
- **Specific figures are [to verify]:** "Daedalea sp. ZnO ~14.6 nm" [4]; Padina pavonica ZnO photocatalysis [8].
- **Safety and contradiction.** ZnO and Zn²⁺ are antimicrobial and fungitoxic, and the repo already keeps them out of living zones ([living_habitat_life_support.md](living_habitat_life_support.md)). Making ZnO *inside* a living *Trametes* composite would inhibit it. Synthesise separately and apply to cured, non-living surfaces. Nanoparticle powders need dust control.
- **Phycocyanin [10].**
  - **Grounded:** aqueous extraction below ~45 °C, freeze-thaw or ultrasound disruption, and the purity index A₆₂₀/A₂₈₀ (food grade ≥0.7, reagent ~3.9, analytical ≥4).
  - **Correction / [to verify]:** "yield up to ~15 mg/g" looks low. Spirulina phycocyanin content is commonly ~10–20 % of dry weight, i.e., ~100–200 mg/g; the figure may be per gram wet biomass.
  - Using it as a UV-stabilising or antioxidant additive in mycelium-hemp composites is **Plausible-untested**. Phycocyanin is itself light- and heat-labile.
- **Coral and biomineralisation [10].** Aragonite skeletons and MICP (ureolytic CaCO₃ precipitation) are **Grounded**. Mycelium scaffolds mineralised by ureolytic bacteria (engineered living materials) are a real research direction. Grok's "Neurospora" example is [to verify]. The ammonia by-product of ureolysis needs handling.

## 4. Plants, sound and light [12][14]

- **Plant responses to sound.** **Plausible**, mixed evidence. Reviews (e.g., Mishra et al., *Plant Signal. Behav.* 2016; Jung et al., *Front. Plant Sci.* 2018) report germination, growth and metabolite changes at ~100–2000 Hz and 70–100 dB. Effect sizes are modest and replication is uneven. "Sonic Bloom" stomatal-opening claims are commercial and **not established** [to verify]. Grok's "~2.2 kHz boosts algal biomass" and "low frequencies enhance mycelium" are [to verify].
- **Bee vision [14].** **Grounded.** Honeybee photoreceptor peaks are ~344, 436 and 544 nm (Peitsch et al., *J. Comp. Physiol. A* 170, 23 (1992)). Bees are red-blind, and UV nectar guides and polarised-skylight navigation are well documented.
- **Plant photoreceptors [14].** **Grounded:** cryptochromes and phototropins (blue/UV-A), UVR8 (UV-B), phytochromes (red/far-red).

## 5. "Light is like water" and the double slit [16][18]

- **Grounded physics.** Interference, decoherence and which-path information; scattering, absorption and re-emission in media; plasma frequency ω_p = √(n_e e²/ε₀mₑ); Boltzmann and Bekenstein–Hawking entropy formulas.
- **Interpretive.** "Measurement selects one outcome" and "no two observers would see the same slice." Interpretations of quantum mechanics differ, and no experiment yet picks between them. Fine as a teaching image, and labelled **Symbolic** as a claim about reality.
- **Correction (minor).** Light in vacuum does not need "constant interactions" to propagate. Its apparent slowing in media (refractive index) does come from repeated interaction with the medium. Channing's image works for light *in matter*, which is how Grok frames it in [18].
- "O⁺ to O⁷⁺ or higher" [18]: oxygen tops out at O⁸⁺ (fully stripped). Trivial.

## 7. Molten glass under vibration [26][28]

- Ultrasonic fining (bubble removal), homogenisation and vibration-assisted forming are researched industrial methods. **Plausible-to-Grounded**; Grok cites no papers, so [to verify].
- "Vibration lowers effective viscosity (shear-thinning)" is **Correction / [to verify]**. Silicate melts are close to Newtonian at forming rates. Apparent viscosity drops under ultrasound come mainly from heating and cavitation.
- Glass + molten iron under vibration [28]: Grok found no direct work, which is honest. Enamelling and glass-to-metal seals are the nearest established practice.
