Core Physics of a Tesla Coil

A Tesla coil is an air-cored resonant transformer invented by Tesla in 1891. Unlike conventional iron-core transformers (which rely on tight magnetic coupling and turns ratio for efficient energy transfer), Tesla coils use loose inductive coupling + resonance to achieve dramatic voltage step-up at high frequencies (typically 50–500 kHz). [Wikipedia]

Key Components & Operation:

Primary Circuit: Low-voltage input → step-up transformer → high-voltage capacitor + spark gap + primary coil (few thick turns).
Secondary Circuit: Tall helical coil (many fine turns) with a top load (toroid) that acts as a capacitor to ground.
Resonance Matching: Both circuits are tuned to the same resonant frequency:
f = 1/(2π√(L1·C1)) = 1/(2π√(L2·C2))
or equivalently L1·C1 = L2·C2. [Wikipedia]

When the spark gap fires, the primary LC tank oscillates and transfers energy inductively to the secondary. Because coupling is loose (k ≈ 0.05–0.2), energy builds gradually in the secondary through resonant rise — like pushing a swing at exactly the right rhythm. The secondary behaves as a quarter-wave helical resonator, producing high voltage (hundreds of kV) but low current at the top, creating spectacular arcs. [Richieburnett]

Resonant Frequency & Coupling:
The dual-resonant system can show "beat" frequencies or split modes (lower and upper peaks) depending on coupling strength. Optimal tuning maximizes energy transfer without destructive interference. Modern solid-state versions (DRSSTC) replace the spark gap with IGBTs or MOSFETs for precise control. [Richieburnett]
