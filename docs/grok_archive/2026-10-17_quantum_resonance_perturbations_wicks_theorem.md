Your body’s primary stress-response command center. It translates perceived threats into

Peptides in the Nervous System
Peptides are short protein chains (typically <50 amino acids) that act as signaling molecules. In the
nervous system they function as:

fine-tune circuits.

Protein Chains: From Sequence to Function
Proteins are long chains of amino acids (polypeptides) folded into precise 3D structures:
Primary structure: Amino acid sequence (determined by DNA/RNA).
Secondary/tertiary/quaternary: Folding via hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic interactions, disulfide
bridges, etc., creating functional shapes (e.g., alpha helices in channels, binding pockets in
receptors).
In the nervous system: Ion channels (precise pores that open/close with voltage or ligands),
receptors, enzymes (synthesizing neurotransmitters), and cytoskeletal elements (microtubules
for transport and structure).
Folding is exquisitely sensitive; misfolding is implicated in neurodegeneration. These structures
enable the speed, specificity, and adaptability of neural signaling.
