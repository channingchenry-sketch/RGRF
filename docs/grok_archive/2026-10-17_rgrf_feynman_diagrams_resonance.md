Core Principles of KNN Phase Boundary Engineering
Pure KNN has an orthorhombic (O) structure at room temperature with phase transitions:
rhombohedral-orthorhombic (R-O) at low temp, O-tetragonal (O-T) around 200°C, and tetragonal-
cubic (T-C) ~400°C. These transitions limit performance away from the boundaries.

Engineering shifts these boundaries to room temperature or broadens them via doping, creating
multiphase coexistence (e.g., O-T, R-O-T, or R-T). This increases polarization directions,
eases domain wall motion, and enhances intrinsic (lattice strain) + extrinsic (domain switching)
contributions — often pushing d₃₃ from ~100 pC/N (pure KNN) toward 300–800+ pC/N in
optimized systems.

Common Strategies:

    Chemical Doping/Substitution:

        A-site (K/Na): Li⁺, Bi³⁺ to shift transitions.

        B-site (Nb): Sb⁵⁺, Ta⁵⁺, Zr⁴⁺, Hf⁴⁺ for phase flattening and local heterogeneity.

        Multi-component solid solutions (e.g., KNN + Bi₀.₅Na₀.₅ZrO₃, CaHfO₃) create "slush" polar
        states or nanodomains.

    Defect Engineering: Acceptor/donor co-doping (e.g., Cu²⁺ defect dipoles) pins domains for high
    Qₘ while preserving d₃₃.
    Microstructural Control: Texturing, grain size reduction, or multilayer designs for temperature
    stability and anisotropy.

Key Examples Relevant to Our Regenerative Design
    O-T Phase Boundary: Achieved with Sb, Zr, or Bi doping. Yields d₃₃ ~300–500 pC/N with good
    thermal stability when diffused.

    ultrahigh d₃₃ (up to ~650 pC/N reported) via polarization rotation and low-angle polar
