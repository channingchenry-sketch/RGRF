Holt himself framed it as speculative. NASA’s own foreword on the memo says the work was

3. John R. R. Searl — SEG and the inverse-gravity vehicle
John Roy Robert Searl (b. 1932) described the Searl Effect Generator from work he dated to
1946–1956:

    Concentric stator rings with cylindrical magnetic rollers that orbit without contact.
    Laminated stacks: magnetic / conductive / dielectric layers, magnetized in a patterned “Law of
    the Squares.”

    Claims: self-spin, excess electrical output, cooling instead of heating, ionization/corona, and at
    high RPM weight reduction or lift. Scaled story: the Inverse Gravity Vehicle.

What subsequent builders have actually shown (Ritchey, Godin/Roschin-style machines,
Morningstar Energy Box, etc.) is more modest: magnetic bearings can run, rollers can be driven to
hundreds of RPM, some runs report small apparent weight changes that have not survived as
accepted, calorimetry-grade overunity. Mainstream constraints still apply: static permanent-magnet
arrangements do not furnish continuous torque without time-varying fields or an energy input;
Faraday pickup coils do not create energy; corona and ion wind can mimic lift.

4. Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou recurrence
Summer 1953, Los Alamos, MANIAC I. Enrico Fermi, John Pasta, Stanislaw Ulam, and Mary Tsingou
(who wrote the code and was long omitted from the name) simulated a chain of masses with weakly
nonlinear springs. Fermi expected the energy in one vibrational mode to thermalize — equipartition.
Instead the energy spread and then nearly returned to the original mode. Quasi-periodic
recurrence, not a heat bath.

Why it matters:

    Continuum limit of the α-chain is the Korteweg–de Vries equation. Solitons pass through each
    other and keep their identity — that is one reason the first note comes back.
    Nearby integrable systems (Toda lattice) protect coherence for a long time.

    The system is not immortal. On much longer timescales, higher-order resonances (four-wave,
    six-wave) do mix the modes. Thermalization time for weak nonlinearity can scale like a high
    power of the coupling — so the “memory” can last extraordinarily long before the lattice forgets.

For us: this is the best established physics among the four for regenerative design. A nonlinear
lattice does not have to dump its ordered energy into heat on the first bounce. Phi-spaced coil
modes, mycelial networks, and coupled resonators can behave more like q-breathers than like a
resistor. FPUT is also why we should not assume “resonance equals free energy.” Recurrence is
conservation plus structure, not a violation of thermodynamics.

The jitterbug is not a second solid sitting next to the vector equilibrium. It is the VE moving. Fuller
built a cuboctahedron with rigid edges and flexible vertices in 1948, pressed two opposite triangles
together, and watched the whole cage twist and shrink through shapes that geometry books treat as
separate objects. He named the motion after the dance.

The only parts that stay themselves are the eight equilateral triangles. The six squares are the
soft joints. They diamond, pinch, and finally close so that twenty-four edges become twelve

What actually moves
Each triangle does a screw: it rotates about its own face-normal while sliding in or out along that
same axis. A vertex does not travel on a circle. Its path is a segment of an ellipse. The usual angular
parameter γ runs from −60° to +60° :

    γ = 0° — fully open vector equilibrium (cuboctahedron)
    γ ≈ ±22.2° — regular icosahedron (the short diagonal of each diamond equals the edge)
    γ ≈ ±30° — Jessen orthogonal icosahedron, the same silhouette as a six-strut tensegrity
    γ = ±60° — octahedron, edges doubled in parallel pairs

Push past the octahedron, twist, and the model flattens into a four-triangle pack that folds into a
tetrahedron. Left-handed and right-handed contractions are both legal; the VE is the rest point
between them.

The icosahedron is a recognized station, not a lock. The dance does not stop there unless you insert
extra struts across the diamonds. The VE and the doubled octahedron do lock: one is maximum
open, the other is edges paired.
