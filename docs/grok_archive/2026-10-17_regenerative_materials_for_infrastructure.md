Fundamental Physics of Alfvén Waves

Named after Hannes Alfvén (1942), these are transverse MHD waves in magnetized plasmas. Ions and electrons oscillate perpendicular to the background magnetic field B₀, with magnetic tension providing the restoring force — like plucking a guitar string where the "string" is a magnetic field line.[Svs.gsfc.nasa]

Key Equation — Alfvén Speed (ideal MHD, incompressible plasma):

v_A = B_0 / sqrt(μ_0 ρ)   [math rendered garbled in source; reconstructed as plain text]

where μ_0 is vacuum permeability and ρ is plasma mass density. This is the characteristic propagation speed along B₀.[Wikipedia]

Dispersion Relation (ideal MHD, parallel propagation):

ω = k_∥ v_A   [math rendered garbled in source; reconstructed as plain text]

Non-dispersive in basic form: phase and group velocity both equal v_A.
Waves propagate only along magnetic field lines (anisotropic).
Incompressible: no density perturbation, pure shear.[Fiveable]

For kinetic Alfvén waves (smaller scales, including electron inertia/pressure): dispersion becomes more complex, with perpendicular wave numbers introducing phase speed modifications and coupling to other modes.[Pubs.aip]

Propagation Characteristics & Real-World Behavior
Polarization & Modes: Shear (transverse) Alfvén waves vs. compressional (fast) magnetosonic waves. In solar corona or cosmic plasmas, they often appear as torsional or kink modes.[Plasma.physics.ucla]
Damping & Instabilities: In partially ionized plasmas (e.g., solar chromosphere), neutral collisions introduce cut-offs. Nonlinear effects lead to decay instabilities, Landau damping, or turbulent cascades that heat plasmas (key to solar corona mystery).[Aanda]
