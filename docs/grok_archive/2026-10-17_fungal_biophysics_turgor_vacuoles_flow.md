In walled cells like fungal hyphae, turgor pressure is the internal hydrostatic pressure generated by
osmotic water influx into the vacuole, pressing the plasma membrane against the cell wall. Typical
values in filamentous fungi range from 0.4–0.6 MPa (roughly 4–6 atmospheres).

Tubular Vacuolar Systems
Unlike the single large central vacuole of many plant cells, filamentous fungi (including
basidiomycetes) possess a highly dynamic, pleiomorphic tubular vacuolar network. These tubules
form longitudinally oriented bundles, are motile (especially near tips), and function as an intracellular
“pipeline” system for:
Long-distance transport of nutrients, ions, polyphosphate, and waste
pH and ion homeostasis
Autophagy and storage
Rapid responses to environmental change (motility can be induced)
Tubular vacuoles are most active in growing tips but can be mobilized throughout the mycelium.
