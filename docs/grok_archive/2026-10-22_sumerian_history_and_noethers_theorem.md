Noether’s theorem, grounded
Emmy Noether (1882–1935) published Invariante Variationsprobleme in 1918. Hilbert and Klein had

Einstein called the work “penetrating mathematical thinking.” She was still barred from a proper
professorship in Göttingen for being a woman. In 1933 she was dismissed by the Nazis and went to
Bryn Mawr. She died after surgery in 1935, age 53.

Informal statement (first theorem):

If the action of a system is invariant under a continuous symmetry, there is a corresponding
conserved quantity. The converse also holds under the technical conditions of the theorem.

The action is

[MATH, reconstructed: S = ∫ L(q, q̇, t) dt]

Physical paths are those that make S stationary (Hamilton’s principle). A continuous symmetry is a
one-parameter family of transformations that leaves S unchanged (or changes L only by a total time
derivative). Noether then constructs a charge Q whose time derivative vanishes on-shell — that is,
when the Euler–Lagrange equations hold.

A compact mechanics version: if a transformation generated by functions a_i(q) leaves L invariant up
to dF/dt, the Noether charge

[MATH, reconstructed: Q = Σ_i a_i ∂L/∂q̇_i − F]

is constant. Cyclic coordinates are the kindergarten case: ∂L/∂q = 0 already means the conjugate
momentum is conserved.

Second theorem: if the symmetry depends on arbitrary functions of spacetime (gauge /
diffeomorphism freedom), you do not get ordinary conserved charges. You get identities among the
field equations. That is why local energy conservation in general relativity is subtle: the symmetry
group is infinite-dimensional. Noether classified the “improper” conservation laws that had
confused Hilbert.

Limits, said plainly:

The theorem applies to variational systems (Lagrangian / action principles), not to every slogan
we call a “law.” The second law of thermodynamics is not a Noether symmetry.
Discrete symmetries (parity, charge conjugation) are outside the original continuous-group
statement.

“Every symmetry gives a conserved quantity” is a half-truth until you specify continuous
variational symmetry.

That is the whole engine of particle physics and field theory: find the symmetry group, read off the
charges, write the allowed interactions.

Arnold’s picture is the clean geometric home for ideal Euler. Gathering the core statements,
curvature/stability consequences, and where it stops for viscous or two-phase cells.
Arnold’s model is the place where “the fluid is a geodesic” stops being a metaphor. It is a theorem
about ideal flow: no viscosity, volume fixed, kinetic energy as the metric. That is the right geometry
for the outer Euler-like phase. It is not the geometry of a clocked two-phase cell. We keep that split.

The 1966 statement
A blob of incompressible inviscid fluid filling a domain M is a point in the group

G = SDiff(M)

of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms of M . Each φ ∈ G is a placement map: particle with label X
sits at x = φ(X). The identity e is “everyone at their label.” Time evolution φ(t) is a curve in G.

Kinetic energy

[MATH, reconstructed: E(φ, φ̇) = ½ ∫_M |φ̇|² dV]

is the squared L² norm of the velocity. Because the energy of a fluid does not care how you named
the particles yesterday, this quadratic form is right-invariant: you right-translate the metric from the
identity across the whole group.

Arnold (1966): the incompressible Euler equations

[MATH, reconstructed: ∂_t u + (u·∇)u = −∇p,   ∇·u = 0]

are exactly the geodesic equations of that right-invariant metric on SDiff(M). Pressure is the
Lagrange multiplier that keeps the curve inside the volume-preserving group.

Same pattern as a rigid body: Euler’s top is geodesic flow on SO(3) with a left-invariant inertia
metric. Fluids are the infinite-dimensional twin, with right invariance because the Eulerian velocity is
the right-trivialized tangent vector

[MATH, reconstructed: u(t) = φ̇(t) ∘ φ(t)⁻¹]

Lagrangian path = group element. Eulerian field = Lie-algebra element.

Euler–Arnold equation
On any Lie group with a right-invariant metric, a geodesic’s Eulerian velocity u(t) ∈ g obeys
[MATH, reconstructed: ∂_t u = −ad*_u u]

(the Euler–Arnold equation). For g = divergence-free vector fields and the L² metric,

[MATH, reconstructed: ad*_u u = P((u·∇)u)]

where P is the Helmholtz–Hodge projector onto divergence-free fields. That projector is −∇p.

Conservation along the geodesic:

[MATH, reconstructed: Ad*_{φ(t)} u(t) = u₀]

— the momentum is Lie-dragged. In hydrodynamics that is vorticity frozen into the flow.

Where Noether sits (this is the join from earlier)
Right invariance is particle-relabeling symmetry: you may compose φ on the right by any volume-
preserving change of labels and the Eulerian kinetic energy does not change. Noether’s theorem for
that infinite-dimensional symmetry is Kelvin’s theorem and the vorticity law:

[MATH, reconstructed: D/Dt ∮_{C(t)} u·dl = 0,   ∂_t ω + L_u ω = 0]

Helicity ∫ u ⋅ ω dV is the Casimir for the barotropic case — a Noether charge for a larger relabeling
family. Potential vorticity is the baroclinic cousin. Time-translation of the action still gives energy;
spatial isometries of M still give momentum. Relabeling is the extra symmetry that only a continuum
of indistinguishable particles has. That is why fluids have more conservation laws than a rigid body
of the same kinetic energy.

So: Sumerian accounting conserved named goods. Relabeling says the names of fluid parcels are
fictitious. Arnold geometrizes that fiction.

Curvature: why nearby parcels diverge
Sectional curvature of SDiff(M) can be negative in many two-planes. Arnold computed this on T²;
later work extended it to 3D (e.g. pairs of ABC flows). Negative curvature means geodesic deviation
grows. In fluid language: two slightly different initial placements of particles separate exponentially
— Lagrangian instability — even when the Eulerian field looks calm.
Caveat that Shnirelman and others keep stressing: curvature sign is not a complete stability
criterion. Positive curvature can also destabilize; flat directions matter; Eulerian vs Lagrangian
stability are not the same question. Arnold’s method of conserved energy-Casimir functionals
(Arnold stability) is the practical nonlinear test for Eulerian equilibria, later used on stratified shear,
vortex patches, MHD. Richardson-number-type criteria live there, not in raw sectional curvature.

Ebin–Marsden made the infinite-dimensional manifold honest: in Sobolev spaces Hˢ with s >
n/2 + 1, SDiffˢ is a smooth Hilbert manifold and the geodesic spray is smooth, so short-time
Lagrangian existence is ODE existence on that manifold. That is existence for Euler in Lagrangian
coordinates, not a solution of Clay NS.
