Bernoulli's principle is a cornerstone of fluid dynamics, named after Daniel Bernoulli (published
in Hydrodynamica, 1738). It stems from the conservation of energy in steady, incompressible,
inviscid (frictionless) fluid flow along a streamline.
Key intuition (Bernoulli's principle): In a flowing fluid, an increase in speed occurs
simultaneously with a decrease in pressure (or potential energy). Faster-moving fluid exerts
lower pressure.
The Bernoulli equation (often in this simplified form for incompressible flow along a streamline):
1
P + ρv 2 + ρgh = constant

Where:
P : Static pressure
ρ: Fluid density
v : Velocity
g : Gravity
h: Height (gravitational potential)
This balances pressure energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy. Leonhard Euler later
formalized it mathematically.

Aerodynamics and lift: Airplane wings (air flows faster over the curved top surface → lower
pressure → lift). Note: Full lift also

Venturi effect/meters: Narrowing a pipe increases speed, drops pressure (used in
carburetors, aspirators).
Everyday demos: Blowing over a paper strip (it rises), or levitating a ping-pong ball in a
hairdryer airstream (high-speed air around the ball creates lower pressure, holding it in the
flow via Coandă effect too).
Engineering: Carburetors, atomizers, chimney drafts, blood flow in vessels, and even some
