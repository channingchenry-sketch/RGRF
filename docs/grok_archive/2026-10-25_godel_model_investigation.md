The original Gödel universe does not expand. Later Gödel-type metrics add expansion; those

Two properties made the model famous:

1. Global rotation. Every local inertial frame co-rotates with the cosmic dust. Foucault
pendulums everywhere precess in unison. That is an explicit affront to a strong reading of
Mach’s principle.
2. Closed timelike curves (CTCs). Beyond a critical radius the azimuthal Killing field becomes
timelike. There are closed worldlines through every event. Time travel into one’s own past is
geometrically allowed. Gödel used this to argue that GR does not force a unique “now.”

Einstein knew the solution. His comment was that a closed causal series makes “earlier / later”
poorly defined.

What it is not
It is not our observed cosmos. Original Gödel has no Hubble flow. Observational bounds on
cosmic vorticity are tight; Hawking already noted that data only allow a very small rotation.
Gödel spent years asking whether the universe was rotating yet and was told no.
Negative Λ is opposite the sign we measure.

CTCs are a feature of this geometry, not a license for time machines in expanding FLRW or
in laboratory hardware.

Superluminal tangential speeds appear far from the axis if rotation is large; that is why
realistic rotation rates, if any, must be tiny.

Treat CTCs as a consistency probe of GR, not as an engineering path.

Grain charge drops, can even flip. Electrons normally charge dust hard-negative. Steal the

What “metal gates” most likely was
Several real setups use metal plates with apertures as gates. None are secret; they just live in
ion-source literature, not dusty-crystal talks.

1. Plasma grid (PG) — the strongest match.

Fusion negative-ion sources extract H⁻ through a metal plasma grid: a plate with many holes,
often aluminum, biased negative. A second control / extraction grid sits behind it. Positive ions
are pulled into the holes, some convert or the surface (Cs-coated) emits H⁻, electrons are
filtered magnetically. Japanese work explicitly uses an Al plasma grid plus a control grid to make
a stable ion–ion plasma downstream. That is literally gated metal.

2. Anode-spot confinement (Feb 2025, easy to miss).

Choi et al. used a metal anode to form an anode-spot double layer in Ar–H₂. Negative ions from
the surrounding plasma flow in and stay trapped. Electronegativity inside the spot rose by up to
20×. Pressure and anode size set the trap. This is the cleanest recent “metal electrode +
electronegative well” paper.

3. Afterglow RF gate (Goree-type).

Not a metal gate — an RF function-generator gate. Plasma on, grains negative and levitated;
power gated off; afterglow ions can leave grains positive. Easy to remember as “they gated it.”

4. Named “plasma gate” patent (US10204765B2).

A chamber with timed electrodes that separates electrons onto a metal surface and steers the
leftover ion plasma. Engineering device, thin on published diagnostics.

5. Not this: plasmonic crystals with metal grating gates, GaN HEMT fluorine-gate process,
graphene side gates. Those are solid-state “metal gates,” easy to collide with in a search.

If the thing you saw had a holey metal plate in front of a glow and a second biased plate, it was
a plasma-grid / extraction stack. If it was a small anode glowing with a sharp boundary and they
quoted a jump in H⁻, it was the 2025 anode-spot paper.

Operating windows (order of magnitude)
Crystal: Ar ~20–40 Pa, RF on, grains levitate in the sheath above the lower electrode. You
want Γ high enough to see hex order on camera.

Bubble: raise anode voltage until a bright, sharp-edged spot appears. That edge is the
double layer. If it flickers and sheds, you are at the plasmoid-train threshold.

Gate: bias the holey plate negative. You are not extracting a beam yet — you are making a
second potential step and a colder/darker region behind the holes.

Pressure: drop a few Pa and the bubble shrinks or dies; raise it and attachment (if O₂ is in)
climbs. That is the p knob.

Temperature zoning: the spot is the hot/high-ϕ well; the gate shadow is the cooler pocket.
No extra heaters on pass 1.
