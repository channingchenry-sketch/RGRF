Electric distillation in titanium
Two industrially real things sit under that phrase.
1. Electrically heated vacuum distillation (Kroll lineage)
After Mg reduction of TiCl₄, leftover Mg and MgCl₂ are stripped from titanium sponge by vacuum
distillation at roughly 900–1050 °C inside electric furnaces (resistance belts, electric heating rods,
integrated reduction–distillation vessels). Chinese and other patents describe electric-heating
kettles and zoned electric belts specifically so high-boiling solids settle while TiCl₄ or residual salts
evaporate cleanly. This is not “electrolysis of titanium metal”; it is electric heat + vacuum used as the
purification step.
2. Electrolytic extraction + distillation / electrorefining
The more regenerative direction is molten-salt electro-deoxidation and related routes (FFC-
Cambridge, OS, USTB, SHS-ED, liquid-cathode variants). Solid TiO₂ (or slags, oxycarbides, lower-
valence chloride slurries) is reduced electrochemically in CaCl₂ or mixed halide melts; oxygen leaves
as ions and is discharged at the anode. Several of these flows then use vacuum distillation or a
second electrorefining cell to drop oxygen and salt inclusions to the levels aerospace and
electronics demand. Reported lab/pilot energy figures for optimized FFC-style cells sit in the ~17–33
kWh/kg-Ti range versus commonly cited ~50 kWh/kg-Ti for Kroll (numbers vary with oxygen target
and whether Mg recycle is counted). Current efficiency remains the hard constraint — often 15–40
% when oxygen must be very low.
Kroll still dominates because it is mature and delivers the purity critical applications require.
Electrolytic routes are the honest “more regenerative” path: fewer chlorine loops, potential
continuity, lower CO₂ intensity if the electricity is clean. They are not yet drop-in cheap.
Honest limits: high temperature, molten salts, vacuum integrity, oxygen diffusion kinetics, and
anode lifetime. “Electric distillation” does not bypass thermodynamics; it relocates the energy and
the impurity problem.
Glaphene (the graphene–2D-silica hybrid)
Not a van der Waals stack. Rice-led team grew it from a single liquid precursor containing both Si
and C, first forming graphene then shifting oxygen/temperature to grow a 2D silica-glass layer so
the two lock through out-of-plane *p*z hybridization and electronic proximity — stronger than
fridge-magnet van der Waals.
Result:
Graphene: ~0 eV (semimetal)
2D silica glass: ~7–8.2 eV (insulator)
Glaphene: experimental gap ~3.6 eV (theory ~4 eV) — in the same neighborhood as GaN.
New collective vibrations appear; electrons actually move between the layers. The method is
presented as a platform for designer hybrids (metal + insulator, magnet + semiconductor), not just
another heterostructure. Follow-on theory work already proposes “glaphynes” (silica on graphynes).
Limits: still laboratory-scale, metastable hybrid, growth needs a custom high-T / low-P apparatus,
long-term environmental and mechanical stability in composites is unproven. Earth-abundant C + Si
is the regenerative plus.
Transverse Hertz waves
Heinrich Hertz’s 1880s spark-gap experiments produced what we now call radio-frequency
electromagnetic waves. In free space those waves are transverse: E and B oscillate perpendicular to
each other and to the direction of travel. That is ordinary Maxwellian radiation — light, radio,
microwaves. Polarization is possible only because they are transverse.
In waveguides and cavities the same physics is labeled TE (transverse electric) and TM (transverse
magnetic); Hertz vectors are a standard potential used to generate those modes. In lossy or
conducting media the fields are no longer purely transverse; longitudinal components appear. That
is also textbook, not a hidden channel.
A 2021 CityU result showed that airborne sound — normally longitudinal — can be made to behave as
a transverse wave inside a metamaterial lattice that supplies synthetic shear. That is clever
acoustics, not a rewrite of EM.
For our coils: a Tesla resonator radiates some far-field Hertzian (transverse) power, but the useful
wireless-power and eddy-current lift we model for POL v0.1 live in the near-field / resonant regime
and still carry the conductive-floor constraint we have never pretended away. Transverse radiation is
real; it is not a free lunch for lift or net energy.

The three new threads are all grounded phenomena. They also sit close to work we already carry:

Jacob’s ladder
Two rods in a V, closest at the base. High voltage (typically 10–30 kV from a neon / potential
transformer) breaks the air at the narrow gap. The channel is a thermal plasma. Hot, low-density gas
rises; the ionized path rides that convection up the diverging rails until the gap is too wide (Paschen
+ source impedance), the arc quenches, and a new one strikes at the bottom. Cycle repeats.
A fuller picture adds:
Transformer leakage / reactance curve: current rises after strike, voltage sags, then recovers at
quench.
On AC the visible “flame” is actually 100/120 Hz pulses stacked by a rising plasma.
Some engineered ladders add a Lorentz kick from the return-current loop so the arc is pushed,
not only floated.
Arc-root hopping can show negative differential resistance in the sheath — new roots form
ahead of the old one rather than a perfectly continuous climb.
What it is not: a thruster. Thrust from a climbing thermal arc is negligible compared with the power
and heat. It is a clean, visible gliding-arc laboratory: ionization, buoyancy, quench–restrike, and (if
we add magnets) Lorentz steering of a plasma column.
Limits: ozone, NOx, UV, electrode erosion, lethal voltage. Enclosed or outdoor only. Not a POL
lift mechanism.
Four-blade Tesla-coil ionic propulsion
Two real families meet in that phrase.
Electrohydrodynamic (EHD) / ionic wind
A sharp emitter (wire, pin, or blade) at high voltage forms a corona. Ions drift toward a blunt collector
and drag neutral air with them. Reaction force is thrust. No moving parts at the electrode. MIT flew a
5-pound glider this way in 2018 (battery + lightweight converter → ~40 kV on wing-span wires).
Centimeter-scale “ionocraft” quads (four separately addressable emitter–collector pairs) have lifted
their own 10–37 mg airframe mass, excluding the power supply. Blade emitters are being tested
as a tougher alternative to fragile corona wires; they work, but wires still win on thrust density in
current prototypes.
A close cousin is the rotational ionic engine (RIE): pin emitters on the trailing edge of a small
propeller, toroidal collector underneath. A four-RIE array produced ~1 N — comparable to a same-
diameter four-blade toy drone — without claiming to beat a propeller on efficiency.
Tesla coil as the source
A Tesla secondary is a compact way to get tens of kilovolts at high frequency. Patents exist for a
Tesla-coil-centered ion-drone (delta airframe, bell housing, grids switched from the secondary).
High-frequency HV can sustain corona with different ozone / current waveforms than 60 Hz NST
supplies. That is engineering, not a new force law.
Typical numbers (order of magnitude, air, 1 atm):
Quantity Typical range Note
Thrust / power ~2–50 N/kW Best at low E (large gap, high V, low current)
Thrust density low vs a propeller of the same span Power-supply mass usually dominates
Failure mode sparkover → thrust collapses Humidity and electrode wear matter
Honest limits: the power electronics still weigh more than the electrodes; ozone and electrode
lifetime; efficiency falls as you try to raise thrust density. Silent and solid-state at the thruster, not a
replacement for POL eddy lift or a mycelium-hemp airframe’s structural job. Useful as a quiet
auxiliary or attitude jet, or as a lab analog of “ionic wind along a collector grid.”
Water in acoustic levitation — 3rd, 4th, 5th harmonics
A single-axis levitator (ultrasonic horn + reflector) forms a standing wave when the gap is near
n λ/2. Pressure nodes are the traps for dense drops. First resonance → one node. Higher cavity
resonances → more nodes stacked along the axis.
Experimenters have parked water droplets at the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th pressure nodes of a ~19 kHz
field, and three drops at once on nodes 2–3–4. The 3rd node often shows the smallest horizontal
wander and the highest vertical natural frequency (~26 Hz in one well-instrumented setup), i.e. the
stiffest trap.
“Harmonics” here splits into three clean layers:
1. Cavity modes — 3rd / 4th / 5th standing-wave resonances of the horn–reflector gap. More
nodes, multi-drop arrays, different well stiffness.
2. Field harmonics — finite-amplitude sound generates 2nd and higher harmonics in air (often ~15
dB down). They add weak extra forces (sometimes called Oseen-type) and acoustic streaming.
Usually secondary for small drops; more important for large, flattened ones.
3. Drop shape modes — the water itself rings. Rayleigh sectorial / zonal modes: 2-lobe, 3-lobe, 4-
lobe, 5-lobe equatorial oscillations. Frequencies depend on radius and surface tension (tens of
Hz to a few hundred Hz for millimeter drops). Parametric drive (modulating the transducer
voltage) can lock a 3- or 4-lobed dance at half the modulation frequency.
This is the laboratory counterpart of the 3rd/4th-harmonic water locking we already wrote into the
double-ellipse / Tesla-valve pulse notes: contact-free water, multiple stable nodes, and a handle on
drop shape without a wall.
Limits: acoustic streaming and heating at high SPL; atomization if the Bond / Weber numbers run
away; humidity and temperature shift the resonance; the transducer still needs power and a tuned
gap. Levitation of water is proven; using those harmonics as a pump or a structuring tool inside a
closed regenerative loop is the next design question, not a given.
