Can a Gas Giant Expel Metals?
This is trickier—direct expulsion of solid metals isn't observed or straightforward, but heavy
elements (metals in astro-speak: anything heavier than helium) do move around via several
mechanisms. Gas giants like Jupiter/Saturn have rocky/icy cores surrounded by metallic hydrogen
layers under extreme pressure.
Core Dissolution: Recent models suggest heavy elements dissolve into metallic hydrogen rather
than staying as distinct cores. Almost all elements mix in under those conditions, blurring
boundaries. No clear "expulsion," but material redistributes.
Envelope Erosion & Impacts: During formation or giant impacts, high-speed collisions can strip
gaseous envelopes, leaving metal-rich remnants (e.g., explaining dense planets like HD 149026b
or TOI-849b). Photoevaporation or Roche-lobe overflow removes lighter gases, enriching
metals.
Atmospheric Escape: Lighter gases escape via thermal, stellar wind, or sputtering processes.
Heavier metals stay bound but can be lofted in storms or lost in trace amounts. No bulk "metal
expulsion" like volcanic plumes—cores are too deep and pressures too high.
Grounded Verdict: Gas giants accrete and retain metals efficiently; expulsion happens indirectly
through envelope stripping or dissolution, not active ejection. In formation disks, rapid gas clearing
after impacts helps create metal-heavy worlds.
