    Appropriate Technology (popularized by E.F. Schumacher in Small is Beautiful): Technology
    designed with deep consideration for the environmental, ethical, cultural, social, political, and
    economic context of the community it serves. It prioritizes small-scale, locally buildable,
    affordable, sustainable, and people-centered solutions over large, centralized, resource-
    intensive systems.
    Open Source Principles: Technologies developed transparently through distributed peer review,
    with designs, plans, and knowledge freely shared (usually under Creative Commons or similar
    licenses) so anyone can use, modify, improve, and redistribute them.

The core promise of OSAT is that anyone can learn how to make and use needed technologies free
of intellectual property barriers, accelerating sustainable development through collaboration rather
than proprietary lock-in.

Key Benefits (Grounded in Research):

    Lower costs and faster iteration via distributed peer review.
    Cultural and environmental appropriateness — solutions fit local resources and needs.

    Resilience through transparency and modifiability.

    printers, open-source water pumps, solar devices, and agricultural tools.

Honest Limitations:

    Quality control relies on community engagement (can be uneven).
