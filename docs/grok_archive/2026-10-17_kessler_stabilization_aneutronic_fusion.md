TAE’s Neutral Beam Injection (NBI) Breakthrough
In late 2025 TAE Technologies achieved the first successful formation of a fully field-reversed

How it works

Energetic neutral beams are injected into a magnetically confined seed plasma. The beams are
trapped, depositing toroidally directed energetic ions that build plasma current. Within ~10 ms the
topology flips from open field lines to a closed FRC. Magnetic reconstructions, fast-ion orbit

    The earlier theta-pinch formation sections (and associated divertors) can be removed.

Field-Reversed Configuration Thruster Prototypes
FRC plasmoids are being developed as pulsed, electrodeless thrusters — often called RMF-FRC or
ELF (Electrodeless Lorentz Force) thrusters. A rotating magnetic field drives azimuthal current in a
plasma column sitting in a steady axial bias field. When the driven current is strong enough, the field

reverses on axis, forming a self-contained FRC plasmoid that is then accelerated by the Lorentz
force between the plasma current and the radial component of the bias field.

Recent experimental progress

    A 2025 Chinese prototype (Huang et al., Plasma Science and Technology) used 210 kHz RMF
    with peak currents of 2 kA. It achieved clear field reversal (155 G against a 60 G bias, optimized
    at 100 G bias), plasma density rising from ~5×10¹⁷ to 2.2×10¹⁹ m⁻³, ~53 % RMF coupling
    efficiency, and 1.9 kA plasma current. High-speed imaging captured plasmoid formation and
    ejection.

    University of Michigan PEPL work has demonstrated continuous/repetitive pulsing, coupling
    efficiencies rising above 50 %, and direct inverted-pendulum thrust measurements.

    MHD simulations indicate that current low performance is largely a power-scaling issue. At
    hundreds of kW to MW levels, with stronger bias fields (>1 kG) and higher RMF
    amplitudes/frequencies, specific impulses above 5 000 s and useful impulse bits become
    plausible.

Advantages for regenerative craft

Electrodeless operation (long life, no grid erosion), propellant flexibility (including potential ISRU
gases), and the ability to produce high thrust density in a compact package. These traits align well
with the kind of high-Isp, modular propulsion options we consider for Waverider / Filament Rider /
Resonance Shuttle concepts.
