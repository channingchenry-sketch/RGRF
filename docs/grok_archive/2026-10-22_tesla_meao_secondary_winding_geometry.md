Clarifying Refraction and “Slowing Time”
Refraction occurs when light passes from one medium to another (e.g., air to glass or mycelium
composites), changing direction and speed due to interactions with atoms/electrons.

     What actually happens: In vacuum, photons travel at c (~3×10^8 m/s). In a medium, the
     phase velocity or group velocity effectively slows (refractive index n > 1) because photons
     are repeatedly absorbed and re-emitted by atoms with tiny delays. The light wave’s
     wavefront bends according to Snell’s law: n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.

     Time aspect: This is not relativistic time dilation. Time dilation (special or general relativity)
     affects clocks moving near c or in strong gravity—proper time slows for the
     moving/gravitating observer. Refraction doesn’t “slow time” globally; it delays the light’s
     propagation through the medium. No causality violation—photons still respect c locally.
