What Is Chiral Superfluorescence?
Superfluorescence (SF) is a many-body quantum optics phenomenon where an ensemble of
quantum emitters (like excitons in a material) spontaneously develops macroscopic coherence. They
emit a short, intense, directional burst of coherent light—much brighter and more organized than
ordinary fluorescence. It starts from an incoherent state and builds through interactions with the
electromagnetic vacuum, leading to phenomena like Burnham-Chiao ringing (oscillatory dynamics)
and quadratic intensity scaling with emitter number.
Chiral superfluorescence adds handedness: the emitted light becomes circularly polarized, with the
polarization direction (left- or right-handed) determined by the material's chirality. This had been
predicted theoretically but was only recently demonstrated experimentally in 2026.
