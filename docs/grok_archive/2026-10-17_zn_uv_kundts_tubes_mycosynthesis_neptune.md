Kundt's Tube: Visualizing Standing Acoustic Waves & Resonance
Kundt's tube (invented 1866 by August Kundt) is a classic apparatus for demonstrating and
measuring acoustic standing waves. A sound source (speaker, piston, or resonating rod) excites a
column of air (or gas) in a transparent tube lightly dusted with fine powder (typically lycopodium). At
resonant frequencies, standing waves form: dust collects at nodes (points of zero net displacement)
and clears at antinodes.
The distance d between consecutive nodes gives the wavelength:

λ = 2d
Speed of sound follows directly:

v = fλ
where f is the driving frequency (known from the source). For air at room temperature, v ≈ 343
m/s; node spacing at 1 kHz is roughly 17 cm. Variants measure speed in solids (longitudinal waves in
a rod) or use microphones/impedance tubes for absorption coefficients.

What Is Mycosynthesis?
It is the biological synthesis of NPs (metallic, oxide, etc.) using filamentous fungi, yeasts, or their
cell-free filtrates/extracts. Fungi excel here due to:
High metal tolerance and bioaccumulation ability.
Abundant extracellular secretion of enzymes (reductases, laccases, peroxidases) and
biomolecules (proteins, polysaccharides, quinones, secondary metabolites).
Ease of handling and potential for scale-up on low-cost or waste substrates.
Common NPs include Ag, Au, ZnO, CuO, Fe oxides, and others. ZnO NPs are especially relevant to
us because of their wide bandgap (~3.3 eV, strong UV absorption), piezoelectric properties,
photocatalytic activity, and antimicrobial effects—tying directly into our Zn/UV discussion and

Mechanisms (Primarily Extracellular)
1. Fungal biomass or (preferably) cell-free filtrate is prepared from cultured mycelium.
2. Precursor salt is added (e.g., zinc acetate or ZnSO₄ for ZnO).
3. Biomolecules reduce metal ions and cap the forming NPs, controlling size, shape, and
preventing agglomeration. A “metabolite corona” (proteins, amino acids like methionine,
riboflavin, etc.) often stabilizes the particles.
4. Conditions (pH, temperature, precursor concentration, incubation time) tune the outcome. Many
protocols use mild heating (~room temp to 100°C) and near-neutral pH.
5. NPs are harvested via centrifugation, washing, and drying; characterized by UV-Vis
(bandgap/plasmon peak), XRD (crystallinity), SEM/TEM (morphology/size, often 10–50 nm or
clusters), FTIR (capping agents), and EDX (elemental composition).

What Is UV Light / Photochemical Synthesis?
This involves using ultraviolet light (typically UVA/UVB or specific wavelengths) to initiate or
accelerate nanoparticle formation:
Photochemical reduction: UV excites electrons in precursors, sensitizers, or bio-extracts,
generating reducing species (e.g., radicals or solvated electrons) that convert metal ions (Zn²⁺,

Photo-assisted green synthesis: Combined with plant/fungal extracts or mild hydrothermal
conditions—UV enhances yield, controls morphology, or improves crystallinity without harsh
chemicals.
Post-synthesis applications: Many green-synthesized ZnO NPs are then tested/activated under
UV for photocatalysis (degrading pollutants via electron-hole pairs).
For ZnO NPs (key for us): Wide bandgap (~3.3 eV) makes them highly responsive to UV. Protocols

Phycosynthesis: Algae-Mediated Green Nanoparticle Synthesis
Phycosynthesis (or algal-mediated synthesis) uses microalgae, macroalgae (seaweeds), or their
extracts to produce nanoparticles. Algal biomass or cell-free extracts supply polysaccharides (e.g.,
alginate, fucoidan), pigments (chlorophyll, carotenoids), proteins, and polyphenols that act as
reducing, capping, and stabilizing agents—similar to fungal enzymes in mycosynthesis but with
added photosynthetic and CO₂-sequestering benefits.
Common targets: ZnO NPs (highly relevant), Ag, Au, TiO₂, etc. Marine brown algae like Padina
pavonica have been used successfully for ZnO with strong photocatalytic performance (e.g.,
methylene blue degradation under light). Freshwater green algae (Spirogyra hyalina) and others also
yield well-characterized ZnO.
Typical Process:
Harvest/culture algae → prepare aqueous extract (drying, grinding, boiling or sonication).
Mix with metal precursor (e.g., zinc acetate/nitrate).
Incubate (room temp to mild heat; sunlight or UV can assist photoreduction).
NPs form via bioreduction; harvest, wash, dry.
Characterization: UV-Vis (bandgap peak), XRD (crystallinity), SEM/TEM (size/morphology, often
spherical 10–100 nm), FTIR (capping biomolecules).
Mechanisms: Biomolecules donate electrons to reduce Zn²⁺ → ZnO nucleation/growth. Algal
metabolites provide a natural corona for stability. UV/sunlight can enhance via photoexcitation
