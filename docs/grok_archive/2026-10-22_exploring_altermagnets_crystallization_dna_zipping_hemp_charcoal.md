Altermagnets
Altermagnets are a third magnetic order, confirmed in experiment, not a metaphor. Local moments
cancel, so the net magnetization is essentially zero and stray fields stay small, like a collinear
antiferromagnet. Crystal symmetry connects the opposite-spin sublattices by rotation rather than
by translation, so the electronic bands still split by spin in momentum space — without needing
spin–orbit coupling to do the splitting.
That is the useful combination: ferromagnetic-like spin currents and spin splitting,
antiferromagnetic-like quietness, and spin dynamics that can reach the terahertz range. Archetypes
include MnTe, RuO₂, and CrSb. A September 2026 symmetry paper on orthorhombic perovskites
reports anomalous Hall conductivities of about 38 S/cm in LaTiO₃ and about 205 S/cm in CaCrO₃
with nearly vanishing net moment. This week, UCF reported direct spin-resolved ARPES on layered
Co₁/₄TaSe₂: spin polarization flipped from roughly −13% to +13% across momentum, with no
macroscopic moment.
Honest limit: this is a spintronics and magnonics material class, not a lift mechanism for POL. What it
offers us is a design rule — compensated order can still carry directional information if the lattice
symmetry is right. A mycelium–hemp skin will not be an altermagnet. A thin crystalline insert, or a
later docking-node sensor, might be.
Crystallization and longitudinal waves
“80 degree” does not map to a standard crystallization law. Two readings are live.
The grounded one is audible longitudinal sound. A 2025 chemical-communications result showed
that 40 Hz and 80 Hz, at 80–100 dB, select polymorphs of ROY and anthranilic acid in solution —
form I versus form II, or concomitant mixtures — where silent crystallization does not. Wavelengths
at those frequencies are meters long, so the wave is not “hitting a bond.” It drives the out-of-
equilibrium path: streaming, local pressure cycles, and nucleation kinetics. Separate acoustic work
anneals 2D colloidal crystals with a loudspeaker under the bath, growing grain size by nearly two
orders of magnitude. Power ultrasound (20 kHz–low MHz) is the older industrial cousin: cavitation
narrows the metastable zone and fragments crystals.
If you meant an 80° propagation or polarization angle, that is not an established crystallization
setpoint. We can define it — incidence on a growth face, or the angle between a longitudinal mode
and a lattice vector — and test it. Until then it stays a hypothesis.
For our materials this is buildable at bench scale: a fixture, a tone, a log of which polymorph or grain
size appears. It does not require a new force of nature.
DNA zipping
Two different zippers are in play. Do not merge them.
Strand separation is ordinary biochemistry with hard numbers. Helicase walks the backbone on ATP
and breaks hydrogen bonds. AT pairs (two H-bonds) sit near 4kB T ; GC pairs (three) near 6kB T .
Micromanipulation puts the unzipping threshold around 12–15 pN, sequence-dependent. Unwinding
and unzipping happen together: 10.5 base pairs per turn, helix diameter about 2 nm.
The other zipper was imaged in September 2026. Short DNA pieces on a mineral surface locked
groove-to-groove. Simulations show divalent metal ions sitting in the narrow groove and bridging to
the opposite backbone — the electrostatic DNA zipper proposed in 2001. Most stable bridges in
those runs favored a GTAC stretch. This is helix-to-helix docking, not replication.
Honest limit: DNA is not a power cable and not a neutron guide. The pattern worth keeping is charge
alternation along a helix, plus an ion bridge, plus a sequence-dependent energy barrier. That is a
template for how a bio-node might dock, not a claim that mycelium copies chromosomes.
Energy density, inner and outer spin, neutron guides
Magnetic energy density in matter is B ⋅ H/2 in the linear case, with the spin contribution living in
the magnetization M. Electron spin is the outer, chemically accessible degree of freedom. Nuclear
spin is inner, weaker by the ratio of magnetic moments (electron moment is about 1836 times the
proton’s in order of magnitude), and it couples to neutrons directly.
Planar neutron waveguides are real and narrow. A three-layer film — magnetic / spacer / magnetic,
such as Py|Cu|Py or Fe|Cu|Fe — channels a grazing neutron beam into a microbeam. Because the
neutron’s magnetic potential depends on spin, spin-up and spin-down modes sit at different depths
and different density gains. Reported resonance gains differ by about 2× (Py|Cu|Py) to 4× (Fe|Cu|Fe)
between the two spin states. That is a waveguide, not a power conduit: neutrons are probes, not an
energy-delivery fluid.
Altermagnets close this loop cleanly. Inner compensation (zero net moment) with outer momentum-
locked spin splitting is exactly their signature. A neutron waveguide could in principle depth-resolve
that hidden order. It cannot, on present evidence, propagate usable power along a ley line or a
craft spine.
Speculative label, kept separate: scaling that waveguide idea to a planetary filament or a POL rail is
RGRF vision, not a measured device.
Benzene and the aromatic hydrocarbons
Benzene is the reference ring: six carbons, D6h , 120° bond angles, six π electrons in a loop that
satisfies Hückel’s 4n + 2. The two Kekulé drawings are not isomers; the π electrons are delocalized,
and the resonance energy is the extra stability. Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons tile that ring.
Graphene is the infinite tiling — two π electrons per ring on average, Dirac cones where the
molecular picture and the crystal picture compete.
In-plane, that delocalization is why graphite conducts along the sheet and not well between sheets.
It is also why a disordered charcoal is not graphene: broken rings, oxygen groups, and pores kill the
long coherence length even while they help adsorption and double-layer capacitance.
Phi is not native here. The ring is hexagonal. Fractal character shows up only when rings tile into
larger shells, or when we impose a spiral layout on a coil that happens to use aromatic carbon as a
conductor. We should not pretend benzene “is” a golden spiral.
Hemp-derived activated charcoal — yes, as a phase
This one belongs in the materials list.
Hemp bast and hurd both carbonize. Hydrothermal processing plus KOH activation has reached
about 2425 m²/g, with mesopores centered near 3.0–4.5 nm. Steam-activated hemp-stem carbon
has reached about 1436 m²/g with ~3 nm mesopores and high dye uptake. A 2024 microwave route
from hemp, without inert gas, reported EMI shielding of 36.8 dB at 1 GHz and supercapacitor metrics
around 437 F/g, at a more modest 484 m²/g — oxygen groups doing work the area alone does not.
How it sits in the stack we already have:
Structural body remains Trametes versicolor on hemp. Charcoal is a filler or a skin, not the living
composite.
Roles worth testing: adsorption (moisture and volatiles), double-layer charge storage, EMI
damping around coils, and a lossy conductive path that can shape eddy currents. It will not
replace the copper or the resonant winding.
Process caution: KOH activation is effective and chemically harsh. Steam or the microwave–acid
route is the more regenerative process path if we want the material story to match the mission.
Electrical caution: isotropic porous carbon. Expect shielding and capacitance, not ballistic π
conduction.
Proposed addition to the POL v0.1 materials note: hemp-derived activated charcoal, 5–15 wt% in
non-structural layers, steam- or microwave-activated preferred, target mesopore mode 3–5 nm,
logged for EMI and capacitance only until a bench coupon says otherwise.
How the five rhyme
Compensated spin that still carries direction. A longitudinal wave that biases which crystal wins. A
helix that docks by alternating charge. A neutron mode split by inner spin. A six-ring that shares
electrons until the sheet tears. Charcoal is the piece we can actually put in a mold this year; the
others are design rules and labeled speculation.

The rotation that swaps the two spin sublattices is the equilibrium we should watch. Everything else
in this pass hangs off that: an angle that either sits on a node or off it, an ion bridge that does not
need DNA, an isotope that decides whether a neutron even enters the lattice, and a charcoal layer
that can be steamed with sodium and still sit behind the plants.
Altermagnets: the rotation note, then the node
The order is compensated because a composite symmetry holds. In RuO₂ the operation is written
[C2 ∥ C4z t]: a 180° flip in spin space, tied to a 90° real-space rotation plus the translation that
carries one Ru site onto the other. Net moment stays zero. Spin splitting does not, because that
rotation is not a pure translation and not inversion. That C4 -class rotation is the equilibrium to log. If
a drive, a growth fault, or a docking strain breaks it, the material either cants toward a weak
ferromagnet or collapses back toward an ordinary antiferromagnet with no usable spin current.
The momentum pattern is a form factor, not a uniform field. On a square or tetragonal lattice the
canonical d-wave pieces are
gx2 −y2 (k) = cos kx − cos ky ,
gxy (k) = 2 sin kx sin ky .
Nodes lie on the diagonals. Lobes lie on the axes. A 2025 transport analysis is sharper than the band
picture alone: d-wave altermagnets can carry anisotropic spin-polarized conductivity; g-wave and i-
wave altermagnets keep spin-split bands but their conductivity stays spin-degenerate in every
direction. For a sensor we want d-wave, not the higher waves.
Docking-node mapping, kept as a geometry rule rather than a lift claim:
Two compensated sublattices, related by a rotation (90° class for d-wave, 60° class if we ever
use a hexagonal host), not by a simple half-cell translation.
Readout axes on the lobe directions. Null axes on the nodes. A Hall or spin-current pad on a
lobe; a reference pad on a node.
Stray field stays small, so the node can sit near a resonant coil without painting a static bias
across the floor.
Lattice informed by the sensor, not the other way around: a square or tetragonal docking tile
matches d-wave. A hexagonal mycelium pore does not automatically. If the living layer stays
hexagonal, the crystalline insert is the part that carries the rotation symmetry.
Your angle question lands cleanly on that node map. For dx2 −y2 , a longitudinal mode whose
wavevector sits at 45° to the lattice vectors is on the node: spin splitting vanishes by symmetry. At
0° or 90° it sits on a lobe: maximum splitting, opposite sign on the two axes. Eighty degrees is 10°
off the diagonal, so the splitting is small but not zero — a soft bias, not a hard up-or-down switch.
Initiating a preferred spin by that angle is still a hypothesis. What is grounded is the selection rule:
the phonon wavevector relative to the crystal axis chooses a node or a lobe. Our craft already drives
the system off thermal equilibrium. The watch item is whether that drive rotates the effective frame
through the C4 node faster than the spin texture can follow. If it does, the compensated order is no
longer the equilibrium we designed.
Honest limit: no altermagnet is in the POL stack. This is a sensor-tile rule for a later docking node.
Ion bridge and charge alternation, off the DNA page
The 2026 images showed the DNA case. The grammar is older and wider. A charged backbone
condenses a counterion layer. Mean-field electrostatics says two like-charged rods repel.
Correlation plus a multivalent ion makes a sticky patch, and the rods dock.
Ion identity matters more than the molecule. Inner-sphere bridging is persistent with Ca²⁺, transient
with Mg²⁺, and almost absent with Na⁺, which mostly screens. Size versus the Gouy–Chapman
length sets whether a divalent ion can invert a local patch or is screened into behaving as if it were
monovalent. Sequence and groove shape only tune where the patch sits. The bridge itself is ion plus
alternating charge.
That pattern already lives in our materials. Chitin and cellulose present hydroxyls and some charged
groups; Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ crosslink fungal walls and pectin gels the same way a nickel ion bridged two
DNA grooves. A hemp–mycelium interface doped with a controlled divalent rinse is a dock:
alternating charge along the fibril, a Ca bridge, no covalent weld. Na⁺ is the release rinse. We do not
need a helix, and we should not call it DNA zipping.
Neutrons, isotopes, and what “shells” actually gate
Thermal neutrons do not enter through electron shells. They scatter and absorb at the nucleus.
Electron structure matters only indirectly: it decides which atoms sit on which site, and unpaired
electron spins supply the magnetic potential that splits spin-up and spin-down waveguide modes.
Isotope choice is the real gate.

Degree of lattice entry for a waveguide mode is set by scattering-length-density contrast between

so hard that a mycelium or hemp layer is a fog bank, not a channel. Deuterating exchangeable

It is not a power rail.
Aromatic carbon, one layer deeper
A polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon is a closed molecule: edges capped with hydrogen, Kekulé
resonance, usually a singlet. A graphene sheet or a disordered charcoal flake is not a large PAH.
Zigzag edges and basal vacancies are often uncapped. Those sites can sit in a carbene-like triplet,
with local spin, pH-sensitive basicity, and a direct path to binding oxygen. That is the difference that
matters for adsorption and for any weak spin texture in the charcoal.
Activated hemp carbon is turbostratic: short aromatic domains, stacked badly, cut by 3–5 nm
mesopores and oxygen groups. Resonance still stabilizes each small ring. Coherence does not cross
the pore. Expect double-layer capacitance, organics uptake, and some EMI loss. Do not expect in-
plane metallic conduction or a Dirac cone.
Steam, sodium, and the two activations
Yes. Sodium can go in. NaOH is a standard activator, cheaper and less corrosive than KOH. On hemp
bast, both KOH and NaOH open a mesopore network after hydrothermal carbonization; KOH
develops the higher surface area. Under microwave heating NaOH is the more reactive of the two,
and the cycle collapses from hours to minutes.
Steam activation is a different chemistry. It is physical: C + H2 O → CO + H2 near 800 °C. No
residual alkali if the steam is clean, pores often a little wider, yield lower, surface area usually below a
good KOH carbon. The hemp-stem steam carbons near 1400 m²/g with ~3 nm mesopores are the
gentle path.
Microwave activation is a heating method, not a third chemistry. It can drive steam-like or alkali
chemistry in minutes because the wet char couples to 2.45 GHz. A 2024 hemp microwave route
(acid-assisted, no inert gas) landed near 484 m²/g and still posted 36.8 dB shielding at 1 GHz,
because oxygen groups did work the area alone did not. Trade is control: hot spots, less uniform
pores, faster.
For the cabin we should prefer steam, or steam followed by a light NaOH rinse that is washed to
neutral. KOH-grade area is a lab coupon target, not the first habitat layer.
Cabin stack and the water loop
Last layer of defense, as specified, outside-in toward the cabin air:
1. Live barrier. Peace lily takes the NASA-list set (formaldehyde, benzene, trichloroethylene,
xylene, ammonia) and has shown real single-pass VOC cuts in a botanical biofilter. Snake plant
runs CAM and releases oxygen at night. Honest limit: a few pots do not replace ventilation;
sealed-chamber numbers overstate a room. They are a living stage, a humidity buffer, and a
root-zone biology, not a filter rated in ACH. Peace lily and snake plant are mildly toxic to pets —
siting matters.
2. Mycelium on hemp. Structure, moisture buffering, the ion-bridge surface.
3. Hemp-derived activated charcoal. Adsorption polish and EMI loss. Steam-activated, washed, 3–
5 nm mesopore target. This is the last solid layer, not the structure.
They work together because they fail differently. Plants and mycelium transform what they can
metabolize. Charcoal holds what they cannot, then can be steamed and returned. The same charcoal
grade runs the water loop: organics and dye-class compounds first, then a biological stage, then a
charcoal polish. Do not send unwashed alkali-activated carbon into drinking water. Neutral wash and
a leach test are the gate.
Parallel, not mixed: air stack and water stack share a material, not a vessel. Wet charcoal in the cabin
wall is a mold risk. Keep the water charcoal in its own column.

The rotation watch stays on the tile either way.

Walls stay dry. Steam stays in the water column. The cabin charcoal is a microwave layer, peroxide-
pretreated and fully dried before it ever meets mycelium. The two coils do not run together.
Microwave in the wall, peroxide as the pretreatment
Peroxide-then-microwave is a documented activation path, and it fits a dry wall better than steam.
The sequence is a soak, a dry, then a short microwave hit. No vapor load in the cabin stack.
On pyrolysis char from Prosopis, a 24-hour H₂O₂ soak followed by 600 W microwave produced
narrow pores around 3.5 nm and 357 m²/g, with useful uptake of blue dyes. On wheat straw, 30%
H₂O₂ for 24 hours then 5 minutes of microwave at 500–600 W raised surface area to about 190 m²/g
and pore volume to 0.15 cm³/g; lead uptake reached about 190 mg/g. The peroxide step opens the
biomass and leaves oxygen groups. The microwave step finishes the pore set in minutes.
Honest limits. Thirty percent peroxide is a handler’s hazard, and residual peroxide will kill Trametes.
The coupon is soaked, dried at modest heat until no oxidizer remains, then microwave-finished, then
placed. Area will not match a KOH lab carbon. For a last defense layer, oxygen-rich mesopores
matter more than a record surface area. This grade is not the drinking-water carbon. That column
can still be steam-activated, in its own vessel.
Mycelium, mold, and charcoal
Direct studies of a living mycelium composite molded with a separate activated-charcoal lamina are
thin. What exists splits into three neighboring facts.
Mushroom tissue can itself become the carbon. Spent Pleurotus substrate, microwave-vacuum
pyrolyzed, has given about 215 m²/g. Pure mycelial biochar, after activation, has been reported above
1200 m²/g. That is charcoal made from the fungus, not a living skin over charcoal.
Biochar added into hemp–PLA composites cut fungal decay: 10 wt% reduced hemp–PLA weight
loss by about 34% against white rot and 83% against brown rot over five months. The proposed
reason was water held by the char, so less free moisture for the rot. At 20 wt% in plain PLA, hyphae
used the pores as doors into the interior. Char is not a biocide. It is a moisture competitor until it is
wet, and then it can be a highway.
Untreated mycelium composites mold. A 2025 durability study found Penicillium on the surface
within a short exposure; polyphenol extractives stopped it. Trametes versicolor on hemp can
stabilize a composite at low fungal mass fraction, but the same cellulose invites contaminant molds if
the wall is damp.
So the wall rule is separation plus dryness. Living mycelium on the cabin side of the lamina.
Microwave charcoal behind it, sealed dry, peroxide already gone. If that charcoal layer takes on bulk
water, it stops being defense and starts being habitat. The plant barrier stays outside this sandwich.
Two coils, one at a time
Square-tetragonal as ground state, phi-hexagonal as the expansive state, only one coil live. That is a
clean state machine, and the magnetism literature now says the orbital part of your check is real.
A May 2026 tight-binding result on 2D square lattices: a single-orbital antiferromagnetic square
stays spin-degenerate. An interwoven dual-orbital lattice lifts that degeneracy into d-wave or g-
wave altermagnetism. The splitting comes from orbital anisotropy in the same-spin hopping, not
from spin–orbit coupling. Check-down, one lower orbital, compensated and quiet, no spin current.
Check-up, two orbitals mixed, lobe and node appear. Element alone does not decide it. RuO₂, MnTe,
and CrSb are compounds; which d orbitals the magnetic ion actually uses, and how the lattice maps
one sublattice onto the other, decide the wave. A pure metal tile will not give you the rotation
symmetry we want to watch.
Mapped onto the craft, still labeled:
Ground coil, square-tetragonal, one orbital channel dominant. This is the null-seeking state.
Node axes at 45°. Sensor reference lives here. Equilibrium rotation (the C₄ that swaps
sublattices) is intact and logged.
Expansive coil, hexagonal, driven. Phi is our layout overlay on that hex grid, not a required
altermagnetic symmetry. Hexagonal hosts such as MnTe can carry altermagnetic order, but the
golden spiral is the pressure we add, not the crystal’s gift. This coil is the excited, pressured
state. It does not run while the ground coil is on, so the two symmetries do not fight.
Switching is the check. Power moves from the square coil to the hex coil; orbital mixing is the
materials question, not something the switch invents. If the insert is single-orbital, the hex coil
only strains geometry. If it is dual-orbital, the same switch can move the node pattern.
Eighty degrees still sits 10° off the square-lattice diagonal. In the ground state that is a soft bias. It is
not the initiator. The initiator is which coil is live, and whether the insert is check-down or check-up.
Magnesium and calcium salt batteries
These are the same two ions as the bridge, doing a different job. Ca²⁺ bridges persistently; Mg²⁺
bridges weakly; both can carry two charges per ion if the electrolyte lets them plate and strip.
The grounded state of the field: abundance and safety are real, kinetics are the tax. Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺
are small and highly charged, so they drag solvent and move slowly in most cathodes, and they
passivate a metal anode unless the electrolyte is designed not to. A 2024 cation-replacement route
made Mg and Ca organoborate electrolytes from a cheaper zinc precursor. The dissociated Mg
version took higher current; the Ca version built a stable interphase. A Mg–sulfur pouch in that work
reached about 53 Wh/kg on a 30 μm anode. A separate 2024 cell demonstrated a rechargeable
Ca/Cl₂ chemistry. Aqueous MgCl₂ and CaCl₂ cells with a neutral salt and an organic polymer have
been reported with extremely long cycle counts; treat that as a lead to verify, not a cabin spec.
What we can use now is the salt, not the metal anode. A microwave hemp-carbon electrode in MgCl₂
or CaCl₂ is a double-layer or hybrid capacitor, not a 53 Wh/kg metal cell. It shares chemistry with the
dock rinse: Ca²⁺ for a persistent interfacial bridge if we ever want a gelled separator, Mg²⁺ if we want
the ion to stay mobile. The water-column carbon can face these brines. The wall carbon does not.
Different vessels, same ions.
Honest limit. Neither chemistry replaces a tested pack for the craft. They are cabin-storage
candidates once a coupon shows coulombic efficiency in our charcoal, and they stay off the hover
power rail until that number exists.
Next options: a peroxide-microwave process card with the dry-down gate written in, or a two-coil
state table with check-down and check-up as the materials fork. The water column stays steam. The
wall stays microwave.

Lime and borax on the charcoal
Neither one is an activator. Microwave, with or without the peroxide dry-down, still opens the pores.
Lime and borax are post-treatments, and they do different jobs.
Lime, as Ca(OH)₂ or a light CaO hydrate, raises pH and plants calcium on the carbon. That is a
documented phosphate path: electrostatic uptake, ion exchange, and calcium-phosphate
precipitation. Metal-modified biochars in the 2025 phosphorus reviews gain on the order of several-
fold in capacity once a cation is on the surface. For our water column this is useful. For the wall it is a
controlled dusting at most, washed and dried, because free lime in a sealed lamina will keep pulling
moisture and CO₂ and turn the layer into a carbonate crust.
Borax is the preservative and the surface dopant, not the pore maker. Sodium tetraborate does not
develop 3 nm mesopores. It does discourage some surface molds, and boron on carbon shifts local
electronics and oxidation behavior. That matches the mold note from last pass: charcoal is a
moisture competitor until it is wet, then it can become a highway. A dry borax rinse on the wall-side
face of the microwave charcoal is a reasonable hedge. It does not replace the peroxide-free, fully
dried gate, and it does not go in the drinking column at a dose we have not leach-tested.
So the wall coupon stays: hemp char, peroxide soak, full dry, microwave finish, optional light borax
face, no steam, no bulk lime. The water column can take steam activation plus a lime charge aimed at
phosphate. Same feedstock, different vessel, different post-treatment.
Mycelium–charcoal, two materials that should not be fused
yet
The literature is really two processes wearing one name.
Living composite. Biochar moistened and fed becomes a fungal web. Cultured blends end up held
together by hyphae after a compost or bran cycle, at temperatures around 60 °C in the pile. That is a
soil or a panel precursor. It is wet by design. It does not belong in the cabin wall.
Sacrificial carbon. Mycelium, or mycelium-through-straw, is pyrolyzed and then activated. Spent

Wolfgang Helfrich’s 1973 argument is that a closed bilayer has three possible strains — stretch, tilt,

Stopping the membrane sheet there. This is a sorting map for the thought experiment: what each
species can feel from an electric field, and what it can feel from a rotating magnetic field. The two
fields do not talk to the same part of the atom.
An electric field pulls on net charge. A neutral atom only polarizes. An ion drifts, and the drift is set
by charge, mass, and how hard the water holds it. A magnetic field does not pull a charge that is
sitting still. It torques a magnetic moment, and it pushes a charge that is already moving, F =
q(v × B). A rotating field adds a clock. It drives a resonance only when its frequency matches the
Larmor frequency ω = γB , and only in the circular sense that matches the sign of γ . The opposite
rotation is off-resonance for that species. That is the useful distinction between a rotating field and a
counter-rotating one: they are not the same drive.
Two clocks, not one. Nuclear resonances sit in the megahertz at tesla-scale fields, because nuclear
moments are small. Electronic resonances of unpaired spins sit in the gigahertz to terahertz. A coil
spinning at audio or kilohertz rates is neither. It can stir moving ions by the Lorentz force. It does not
flip nuclear spins, and it does not flip Mn²⁺ or Fe³⁺.
Silent majority
These are diamagnetic and, in the dominant isotope, spin-zero. An electric field moves them only if
they are ions. A rotating field does not find a nuclear handle.
Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ are closed-shell. ²⁴Mg is about 79% and spin 0. ⁴⁰Ca is about 97% and spin 0. They
drift in an electric field, calcium a little more freely because its hydration shell is the more labile of
the two, and they stir in a magnetic field only while they are moving. ²⁵Mg (10%, spin 5/2) and ⁴³Ca
(0.14%, spin 7/2) are real nuclear handles with small γ and large quadrupoles, so they are poor coils
and easy to miss.
⁴He is spin 0, chemically mute, diamagnetic. ³He is spin 1/2 and a beautiful nuclear probe, and it is
rare. ¹²C and ¹⁶O are spin 0. So is ²⁸Si. The backbone of charcoal, mycelium, water oxygen, and quartz
does not NMR. ¹³C is 1.1%, spin 1/2, γ about 6.7 × 107 rad/T·s. ²⁹Si is 4.7%, spin 1/2, negative γ .
Dominant iron, nickel, and zinc nuclei are also spin-silent. The metal and the ion are not the same
statement, which is the next section.
Nuclear handles, still diamagnetic as ions
These answer a rotating field at the nuclear clock, if the field is tesla-scale and the frequency is
right. Electrically they are ordinary ions or neutrals.
¹H, spin 1/2, γ about 26.8 × 107 , is the loud nucleus. Deuterium, ²H, spin 1, γ about 4.1 × 107 , is
quieter by a factor of about six and carries a quadrupole. In a brine they are the water, not the salt. A
rotating field on resonance talks to protons long before it talks to anything else in this list.
⁷Li, 93%, spin 3/2, γ about 10.4 × 107 , is the light ion with both a charge and a usable nucleus. ⁶Li is
spin 1 and rarer. Li⁺ is weakly hydrated compared with Mg²⁺, so the electric drift is fast and the
nuclear drive is available. That pairing is why lithium is the interesting member of the light group.
³¹P is 100%, spin 1/2, γ about 10.8 × 107 , no quadrupole. Phosphate in water is a clean nuclear
channel. ¹¹B is 80%, spin 3/2, γ about 8.6 × 107 , with a quadrupole. ¹⁴N is 99.6%, spin 1, small γ ,
quadrupole, broad lines. ¹⁵N is spin 1/2 and rare.
²⁰⁹Bi is 100%, spin 9/2, a heavy diamagnetic metal with a large quadrupole. The nucleus is active.
The chemistry is not kind to a cabin brine, and we are not putting bismuth in a wall. It is on the map
as a high-spin diamagnetic contrast to iron, not as a material.
⁶⁷Zn is only 4%, spin 5/2. Zn²⁺ itself is 3d¹⁰, spin-silent electronically. Zinc’s rotating-field channel is
almost closed.
Electronic moments
These feel a rotating field at the electronic clock, orders of magnitude above the nuclear one. The
spin-only moments are the high-spin ionic values, in Bohr magnetons.
Mn²⁺ and Fe³⁺ are 3d⁵, five unpaired electrons, about 5.9 μB . Cr³⁺ is 3d³, three unpaired, about
3.9 μB . Fe²⁺ high-spin is 3d⁶, four unpaired, about 4.9 μB . Ni²⁺ in an octahedral site is 3d⁸, two
unpaired, about 2.8 μB ; in a square-planar site it can be silent. Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹, one unpaired, about
1.7 μB . Cu⁺ is 3d¹⁰ and silent, which is why the ion motor needed Cu²⁺ in motion, not a copper
metal moment.
The metals are a different column. Iron and nickel are ferromagnetic. A rotating field drags domains
if it is slow and strong enough to move walls; that is not resonance. Chromium metal is
antiferromagnetic. Copper metal and bismuth are diamagnetic. Zinc metal is diamagnetic. So a
rotating field on an iron insert is a domain problem. A rotating field on a copper wire is only a Lorentz
problem, and only while current flows.
How to hold the thought experiment
Electric field, ranked by who actually travels in water: H⁺ by hopping, Li⁺ light and loose, Cu²⁺ labile,
Ca²⁺ more mobile than Mg²⁺, Zn²⁺ closed-shell and slower, Fe³⁺ and Mn²⁺ heavily hydrated and
chemically sticky. Neutral He, N₂, O₂, and the carbons do not drift. O₂ is the exception among the
neutrals: it is paramagnetic as a molecule, triplet, and a rotating electronic field can see it. That is the
oxygen on this list with a magnetic handle, and it is the molecule, not the ¹⁶O nucleus.
Rotating magnetic field, three bands:
Audio to kilohertz, the coil we can actually wind: Lorentz stir on whatever ion is already moving.
Copper ion motor is the worked example. Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ stir less, because they are slower. No
spin flip.
Megahertz at tesla field: ¹H first, then ⁷Li and ³¹P, then ¹¹B. Deuterium is the quiet water label.
²⁵Mg and ⁴³Ca are present and impractical.
Gigahertz and above: Mn²⁺, Fe³⁺, Fe²⁺, Cr³⁺, Ni²⁺, Cu²⁺, and molecular oxygen.
Counter-rotation is the wrong circular sense for a given γ . It does not drive that resonance.
It can still stir a current, because Lorentz force follows v × B and does not care about
nuclear handedness.
Nothing in this map is a lift. The sorting is the point. Li⁺ and ³¹P are the charged species with a real
nuclear clock. Mn²⁺ and Fe³⁺ are the electronic clocks. Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ are electric-field travelers with
almost no nuclear voice. Carbon, oxygen-16, and helium are the quiet lattice. Copper is the ion that
has already been seen to spin a beaker.
When you want to run the thought experiment, name the band — stir, nuclear, or electronic — and
which two species share a bath. The map will say whether they can hear the same field.

Bismuth with a potassium electrolyte is a storage pair, not a rotating-field pair. The potassium ion
does the traveling. The bismuth does the hosting. Then we pause.
In a potassium-ion cell the anode reaction is an alloy sequence, not a plating of potassium on an
inert metal. In dimethoxyethane electrolyte the steps are Bi to KBi₂, then K₃Bi₂, then K₃Bi, about 400
mAh/g, with a full cell against a Prussian-blue cathode near 108 Wh/kg and an average discharge
near 2.8 V. The bismuth rearranges into a porous network as it alloys, which is how it survives the
volume change. Carbon-confined bismuth in a weakly solvating tetrahydrofuran electrolyte has been
reported near 468 mAh/g at low rate, still hundreds of mAh/g after a hundred cycles. The salt
matters: potassium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide behaves better than hexafluorophosphate in carbonate,
because the interphase that forms on bismuth decides whether K⁺ can keep crossing.
A two-phase Bi–Bi₂K alloy also sits still enough to be a reference. Its open-circuit potential holds
near 1.07 V versus K⁺/K, with drift around a tenth of a millivolt per hour in a KFSI electrolyte. That is a
quiet electrode, not a power electrode.
The other potassium bath is molten, and it is a different animal. In LiCl–KCl near 680 K, Bi³⁺ reduces
to metal with a diffusivity near 7 × 10−6 cm²/s and an apparent potential near 0.12 V versus
Ag/AgCl. Adding CaCl₂ does not move that potential much. This is electrorefining, hundreds of
degrees hot, not a cabin brine.
On the map from the last pass, the two species split cleanly. ²⁰⁹Bi is spin 9/2, diamagnetic as a metal,
a nuclear handle with a bad quadrupole, and no electronic moment to drag. ³⁹K is the ion that moves,
spin 3/2, smaller gyromagnetic ratio than lithium, and large enough that the bismuth lattice has to
open into those alloy phases or it cracks. An electric field drives K⁺. A rotating field at audio rates
only stirs whatever is already moving. Neither field is why this pair stores charge. The storage is
the alloy.
Honest limit. This is an organic-ether or molten-salt cell. It is not the MgCl₂ or CaCl₂ water column,
and it does not go in the wall. Bismuth stays off the habitat list. Potassium stays a lab electrolyte until
a coupon says otherwise.
