Nitinol: Shape-Memory Alloy and the Warm/Cold Water Phenomenon
Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Naval Ordnance Laboratory alloy) is a well-established shape-memory
alloy (SMA). Its remarkable behavior stems from a reversible solid-state phase transition in its
crystal structure:
Martensite phase (lower temperature): The crystal lattice is “twinned,” allowing large
deformations without permanent damage. The material feels soft and pliable.
Austenite phase (higher temperature): The lattice shifts to a more rigid, ordered structure. The
alloy “remembers” and returns to its original trained shape.
The transition temperature is tunable by alloy composition and heat treatment—often in the 50–
80°C range for demonstration wires, though medical grades can activate near body temperature.
Classic warm/cold water demonstration:
1. At room temperature or in cold/ice water (below transition), you can bend, twist, or crumple the
wire—it holds the new shape.
2. Immerse it in warm or hot water (above transition, e.g., ~60°C or gently boiling), and it rapidly
snaps back to its pre-set “remembered” form—often with surprising force.
This cycle is repeatable many times, though fatigue eventually occurs. Nitinol also exhibits
superelasticity (pseudoelasticity) at certain temperatures, behaving like a spring that returns from
large strains without permanent set.

Proven applications (grounded engineering):
Medical stents and guidewires that expand at body temperature.
Actuators, eyeglass frames, orthodontic wires, and vibration-damping components.
Temperature-responsive engines or deployable structures.
