The flavin organizer, closer
Think of it as a compact charge-separation engine bound inside a protein pocket.

Parts. Oxidized FAD sits in the photolyase-homology domain. Next to it is a tryptophan ladder. In
Arabidopsis CRY1 the ladder is W400 → W377 → W324, with Asp396 as a proton handle. ATP in the

Cycle.

1. Dark rest: FADox, inactive monomer.

2. Blue / UV-A photon makes FAD*.

3. In about 0.4 ps an electron jumps from the nearest Trp. You now have FAD•− and TrpH•+.

4. The hole hops down the triad in tens of picoseconds. Distance is the first stabilizer:
   recombination gets harder as the charges separate.

5. Terminal TrpH•+ dumps a proton to solvent. Asp396 can protonate the flavin. The signaling state
    is the neutral radical FADH• — in plants it can live more than a minute.
 6. Too much light over-reduces to FADH−, which is inactive. Both radical and reduced forms slowly
    burn back to FADox in the dark, often through oxygen. That dark return is where some magnetic
    assays still see an effect.

triplet recombine at different rates. A magnetic field shifts the mixing between those spin states,
which shifts how much signaling-state flavin you keep after a given pulse. That is the radical-pair
mechanism in its honest, local form. It is chemistry with spin, not a radio to the stars.

What the organizer is not. It is not a black hole. It is not an entangled-pair factory for distant craft. It

Phototropin, close in
Phototropin is a movement receptor. Same blue sky as cryptochrome, different engine.

Architecture. Land plants carry two: phot1 and phot2. Each is a single polypeptide with two LOV
sensors (Light–Oxygen–Voltage, a PAS-family pocket) and a C-terminal serine/threonine kinase.
LOV2 is the main on-switch for the kinase. LOV1 tunes sensitivity and helps the protein talk to itself.
In Arabidopsis, phot1 works at very weak blue; phot2 takes over when the light is strong and runs
chloroplast avoidance. Together they also open stomata and flatten the leaf.

The switch is a bond, not a flying electron pair. In the dark, FMN sits in the pocket without a
covalent tether. A blue photon lifts FMN to a singlet, then — unusually among photoreceptors — the
system crosses into a triplet (the LOV715 band). From that triplet the conserved cysteine attacks
flavin C4(a) and a covalent cysteinyl–flavin adduct forms (LOV390). Flavin N5 is protonated at the
same time. A nearby glutamine flips its hydrogen-bond pattern; that ripple moves through the β-
sheet to the surface; the A′α and Jα helices unfold; the kinase is no longer clamped. Dark recovery is
thermal: the adduct breaks on a seconds-to-minutes clock (oat phot1 LOV2 is on the order of half a
minute at room temperature). Water getting back to N5 is part of what sets that clock.

That is why phototropin feels mechanical when you look at it. One photon → one bond → one helix
unfolding → phosphorylation cascade → the plant moves (bends, parks chloroplasts, opens a pore).
Cryptochrome’s job is more like “how much active receptor remains after the pulse.” Phototropin’s
job is “throw the lever.”

Triplet matters. LOV domains are the photoreceptors in which a long-lived flavin triplet is an
established photocycle intermediate. Searches for radical intermediates between triplet and adduct
have found hints (radical anion, maybe a brief semiquinone), but the productive story that
crystallography and kinetics agree on is: triplet → adduct. That is a different quantum object than
CRY’s spin-correlated FAD–Trp pair. Same flavin family, different use of spin.

What phototropin is not. It is not the canonical plant magnetoreceptor. Some growth and gene-
expression studies still implicate phototropin or phytochrome in field effects, including work that is
not light-gated the way CRY’s radical pair is. That remains unresolved. Do not fold phototropin into
the bird-compass story. Its primary physics is covalent switching and kinase allostery.

Recent quantum-biology movement (2024–2026)
The field did not suddenly prove that plants “think quantum.” It tightened a few mechanisms and
built a few instruments.

Antenna / photosynthesis. The old fight was whether long-lived electronic coherence in light-
harvesting is real or a vibrational ghost. Full microscopic simulations of the Fenna–Matthews–Olson
complex (2025) find excitonic coherences that last on the order of a picosecond at both 77 K and
room temperature — the same window as excitation energy transfer. Approximate models that
coarse-grain the vibrational bath can hide that and then misread spectra. That does not mean a leaf
is a quantum computer. It means wave-like sharing of an exciton can survive long enough to matter
for routing energy.

Cryptochrome dynamics. Two 2025 papers sit next to our flavin-organizer note. Plant AtCry1 can
take a nonadiabatic path through a conical intersection and form the first radical pair in under 10 fs.
Avian Cry4a’s separated pairs are stabilized by protein and solvent reorganization, not by vacuum
isolation. Fast birth, wet cradle. That is the honest quantum of the pocket.

A biological qubit. In 2025 a fluorescent protein was run as an optically addressed spin qubit inside
cells — including E. coli at room temperature — using light, microwaves, and fluorescence. The
design borrows radical-pair logic. It is an instrument, not a claim that cytoplasm is coherent by
default. It does show that spin in a protein can be interrogated in a living host if you engineer
the host.

Reaction-center spins. Purple-bacterial reaction centers still make light-induced spin-correlated
radical pairs. 2025 work treats those pairs explicitly as entangled electron-spin qubits and measures
coherence after the photochemical cascade. Again: prepared by a photon in a structured protein,
not floating free in the cell.

Plant magnetic physiology, still mixed. Wheat under static field showed stronger roots and a
jasmonate / HY5 path to nitrogen assimilation (2025). Tomato under a reduced geomagnetic field
shifted MagR-homolog expression and metabolome. An Arabidopsis preprint links PIEZO, blue light,
and field to root growth. These are organism-level responses. They do not yet pick one molecule the
way Cry4 does in birds. Observation: plants notice fields; the sensor list is not finished.

What did not break through: a demonstrated plant compass, room-temperature long-range
entanglement in tissue, or a new force. The progress is better clocks on old mechanisms, and tools
that can ask spin questions inside a cell.

Physics in use: photochemistry, intersystem crossing, covalent adduct, proton transfer, allostery,

Physics in use: spin-correlated radical pairs, singlet–triplet mixing, Zeeman and hyperfine. The field

nanoseconds to milliseconds. Still laboratory chemistry.

Antenna / picosecond coherence (FMO, PSII).

Physics in use: excitons, vibronic coupling, open quantum systems. Coherence is a routing aid
across a few nanometers for a few hundred femtoseconds to a picosecond. “Quantum efficiency”
here means charge-separation yield, not a qubit algorithm.

Prepared laboratory pairs (Bell photons, engineered protein qubits).

Physics in use: entanglement as a resource under isolation and control. Non-local correlations are
real. They do not automatically exist between a leaf and a star.

Organism and field (growth, clock, magnetophysiology).

Physics in use becomes systems: hormones, gene networks, ROS, ion channels (PIEZO), averages
over noisy cells. A field effect at this scale is a phenotype. Inferring a single quantum sensor from a
phenotype is a hypothesis, not a measurement.

Informed-universe / geometric-information layer.

Physics in use becomes metaphor and framework: information as primitive, geometry as participant,
pairs that were never two. That language is allowed as informed speculation. It is not licensed to
rewrite the LOV adduct or the FMO picosecond. The change at this scale is not a new force
appearing. It is us switching from rate equations to a story about meaning — and we have to say so.

A compact way to hold it:

  Phototropin shows how a photon becomes a shape.

  Cryptochrome shows how a photon becomes a spin-biased count.

  An antenna shows how a photon becomes a shared excitation.

  A Bell pair shows how a preparation becomes a non-local constraint.

  The informed-universe picture asks whether those are the same gesture at different resolutions.
  That question is open. The mechanisms above are not.

What we are simply watching
A leaf owns at least two blue machines. One latches a cysteine to a flavin and moves the body. One
separates charge along tryptophans and can, in some assays, feel a field while it reoxidizes. Recent
work makes the first machine’s mechanics sharper and the second machine’s femtosecond birth
sharper, and it puts a spin probe into a living cell. None of that requires us to climb into cosmology.
When we do climb, the physics we cite has to change name, or we start smuggling.
