# Coral reefs as grown wave barriers

License: CC0 1.0. Evidence labels: **Grounded** / **Plausible-untested** / **Speculative**. Every citation was checked by web search.

## Grounded: reefs dissipate waves and protect coasts
- **Ferrario et al.**, "The effectiveness of coral reefs for coastal hazard risk reduction and adaptation", *Nature Communications* 5:3794 (2014), doi 10.1038/ncomms4794. A meta-analysis of 27 studies:
  - The whole reef reduces wave energy by **97 %** (95 % CI 94–98 %) and wave height by 84 %.
  - **The reef crest alone dissipates 86 %** (74–92 %) of the energy.
  - The reef flat removes 65 % of the remainder, mostly in its first ~150 m.
  - Reef defences can be comparable to artificial breakwaters and cheaper to enhance.
- **Storlazzi et al.**, "Rigorously valuing the role of U.S. coral reefs in coastal hazard risk reduction", USGS Open-File Report 2019–1027, doi 10.3133/ofr20191027:
  - US reefs avert ~**$1.805 billion/yr** in flood damage (2010 USD) and protect >18,000 people annually.
  - **Hawaiʻi: ~$836 million/yr** in averted damage, protecting >6,800 people. Oʻahu and Maui account for most of this.

## Hawaiʻi restoration (verified)
- **DLNR Division of Aquatic Resources: Hawaiʻi Coral Restoration Nursery (HCRN).**
  - A land-based (ex situ) nursery at the Ānuenue Fisheries Research Center, Sand Island, Oʻahu.
  - Created after vessel groundings (M/V Cape Flattery 2005, USS Port Royal 2009) and announced in 2016.
  - Fast-growth protocol: transplant-size colonies in about one year.
  - ~90 % outplant survival reported across Oʻahu sites.
  - In 2019 it reintroduced the endemic *Porites duerdeni* to Kāneʻohe Bay.
- **Kuleana Coral Restoration (West Oʻahu).** A community-led group of fishers, surfers and scientists, active since 2019. It has outplanted 1,500+ corals (NOAA story, 2023) and received a 2023 Bipartisan Infrastructure Law grant for community training.
- **The Nature Conservancy Hawaiʻi.** Co-developed the state Coral Restoration Action Plan, with these HERR Network partner projects:
  - **Kanu Koʻa pilots:** Kaʻūpūlehu in 2023, where 313 corals were rescued and reattached in 2025; Kealakekua Bay in 2024.
  - **Kewalo Basin emergency repair (2024):** 200 corals reattached, with Kuleana and DAR.
- I found **no verified hybrid reef/breakwater pilot in Hawaiʻi**, so none is listed.

## Related grown or engineered approaches
| Approach | Evidence | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Biorock / mineral accretion**: low-voltage DC on a steel frame precipitates CaCO₃/Mg(OH)₂ | The coral restoration systematic review (Boström-Einarsson et al., *PLoS ONE* 2020) calls the results varied and controversial. At Agincourt Reef (Great Barrier Reef) it gave no survival or growth benefit but did produce substrate. In Kenya it caused negative effects in a heatwave (*PLoS ONE* 2024). | Plausible for substrate creation; mixed or negative for coral health |
| **Oyster reef living shorelines** | Morris et al., "Large-scale variation in wave attenuation of oyster reef living shorelines and the influence of inundation duration", *Ecological Applications* 31(6):e02382 (2021). | **3D-printed reef modules** | Frau et al., "Effects of ultra-porous 3D printed reefs on wave kinematics", *J. Coastal Research* SI 75:851–855 (2016), 1:15 flume tests | Plausible-untested at field scale |

## Link to RGRF
- **MICP / mineralising thread** (`open_grow_kit.md`, `mycelium_uses.md`). Reefs and MICP both use biology to precipitate CaCO₃ into a porous, rough, self-renewing structure. The RGRF lesson is that **porosity, roughness and crest height** do the dissipation work, not exotic fields.
- **Nodal structuring.** Reef crests are dense, interlocked framework; reef flats are rough, porous nodes. This parallels the dense-skin / porous-core design in mycelium panels.
- **Speculative:** a grown mineralised (MICP) reef module seeded with coral or oysters. No marine test exists yet, and ammonium by-product release into seawater is a concern.

## Falsifiable test
- In a wave flume, compare three geometrically identical porous modules: plain concrete, MICP-cemented sand, and a mineralised mycelium-scaffold module.
- Measure wave transmission (K_t), reflection and dissipation across steepness and freeboard.
- Then field-deploy for a year and measure biofouling, strength loss and ammonium release.
- Hypothesis: equal-geometry modules give equal K_t (geometry dominates). Any material advantage must show up as better recruitment or self-repair.

## Missing pieces
- Wave-climate data and target site.
- Permitting (state coral protection law, US Army Corps of Engineers).
- Marine durability of mycelium or MICP materials.
- Cost per metre compared with restoration of the natural reef crest.
