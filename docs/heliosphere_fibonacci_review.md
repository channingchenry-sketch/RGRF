## 3. The resonant chamber sim and the VLISM (messages 6–8)

- **Grok's message-6 run:** "Rider/system mass: 85 kg", "Input power: 550 W", "Power delivered: 485.8 W", "Efficiency: 88.3%", at "Frequency: 13.0 MHz". **Grok-reported.** It **contradicts the repo**: the repo's hover estimate is ~10–17 kW per person, so ~0.5 kW cannot hold 85 kg at 0.12 m (see [REBUILD_INDEX.md](REBUILD_INDEX.md)). The 13.0 MHz figure also conflicts with the 13.8 Hz / 13.56 MHz values used in other shares.
- **The same numbers return in message 18** as an "FRC" result: "Settle time: 0.09 s", "Overshoot: 25.0%", "Efficiency: 88.3% | Power delivered: 485.8 W", "143.1 μH inductance", "Mycelium Damping: 28.0 Ns/m". These are identical to the message-6 hover chamber run. So they are not an independent FRC plasma simulation. Message 20 then quotes the same "Target "hover"/confinement height: 0.12 m analog" and "Mass analog: 85 kg". **Not an FRC result.**
- **VLISM:** interstellar helium inflow "~26 km/s" (IBEX, Ulysses) and density ~0.1 cm⁻³ are **Grounded**. The field beyond the heliopause measured by Voyager 1 is ~4–5 μG (0.4–0.5 nT). "~0.3–5 μG" is loose at the low end.
- **"magnetic(gravitational waves)" (message 5):** gravitational waves are not magnetic. They are ripples in spacetime, detected by LIGO/Virgo and, at nanohertz frequencies, by pulsar timing (NANOGrav, already cited in the repo). **Corrected.**
- **"localized gravity(magnetic path though polarization.. maybe?"** (message 7): magnetic or plasma polarisation does not create gravity. Field energy does gravitate in general relativity, but at levels ~10⁻⁴⁰ of anything usable. Grok's own limitation ("Magnetic forces are minuscule") agrees. **Contradicted** as a mechanism.

## 7. Regolith, Mg-Si, aluminium (messages 31–34)

- **Regolith ISRU:** plasma or carbothermal reduction of regolith to extract oxygen and metals is an active research area. **Grounded / Plausible-untested** at scale. Regolith as plasma "seeding" that improves EM stability: **Speculative**.
- **Mg-Si alloys:** density ~1.7–2.0 g/cm³ and Mg₂Si precipitates are correct (**Grounded**). However, Grok recommends magnesium while rejecting aluminium for fire risk. Magnesium is *more* reactive and easier to ignite than aluminium. This is an internal inconsistency.
- **"Aluminum would explode, right?"** No. Bulk aluminium is a standard spacecraft structural metal, and its oxide layer passivates it. The real hazards are fine Al powder (a combustion and dust hazard) and molten aluminium in contact with water (steam explosions). Its ~660 °C melting point is the practical limit near a plasma core. **Corrected:** not explosive in bulk; unsuitable near a hot core because of its melting point.
