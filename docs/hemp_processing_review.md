|---|---|---|

These are micro-scale surface-tension effects. Grok notes they change with scale. |
| Fluidic networks show self-oscillation, bistability and rectification of oscillatory flow (fluidic RLC analogues) | Grounded | |
| "Self-timed tidal flushing … without continuous high-power pumping" | Plausible-untested | **Correction:** a self-sustained oscillation still needs an energy source (a head drop, a pump, or the ultrasound power). The loop can lower pumping power; it cannot remove it. |
| Tune the tidal loop "near the ultrasonic frequency (or a sub-harmonic)" | **Correction** | A flume-scale compliant chamber resonates at ~0.1–10 Hz. 20–40 kHz ultrasound is 10³–10⁵ times higher, so direct coupling is a mismatch. A slow loop would need its own drive, or the ultrasound would be duty-cycle modulated at the tidal rate. |
| Small teardrop into a reversed larger teardrop (closed lotus) acts as a diffuser one way and a nozzle the other: passive directionality | Plausible-untested | This is the diffuser–nozzle valveless-pump principle (E. Stemme & G. Stemme, *Sens. Actuators A* 39, 159 (1993)), which is Grounded. Its diodicity is typically low (~1.1–2) and needs an oscillating drive. The lotus shape is a design choice: test against a plain diffuser. |
