## 1. Hydrodynamics rebuilt from symmetries (effective field theory)

**Grounded.**

**The idea:**
- A fluid is defined by its symmetries, not by what it is made of.
- A fluid at rest breaks spacetime symmetries in the same way an expanding universe does.
- Fluids also have "swapping" symmetries: you can exchange parcels at no energy cost, which a solid cannot do.
- Zooming out (Wilson-style coarse-graining) then gives the Euler equations.
- A doubled, time-reversed (dynamical KMS) symmetry builds in dissipation and thermodynamics, which gives Navier–Stokes plus fluctuations.
- Lucas and collaborators have extended the approach to fracton hydrodynamics.

**Sources:**
- **Popular account:** C. Wood, "Theory of Fluids Enters the 21st Century," *Quanta Magazine*, 17 Aug 2026. I verified that the article exists. URL: https://www.quantamagazine.org/theory-of-fluids-enters-the-21st-century-20260817/
- **Non-dissipative fluids:** S. Dubovsky, L. Hui, A. Nicolis & D. T. Son, "Effective field theory for hydrodynamics: thermodynamics, and the derivative expansion," *Phys. Rev. D* 85, 085029 (2012), doi 10.1103/PhysRevD.85.085029.
- **Dissipative fluids:** M. Crossley, P. Glorioso & H. Liu, "Effective field theory of dissipative fluids," *JHEP* 2017(09), 095, doi 10.1007/JHEP09(2017)095. The companion paper (II) is *JHEP* 2017(09), 096.

**Why it matters here:** the approach gives a principled way to list which terms a ferrofluid, plasma or vortex model is *allowed* to contain, once its symmetries are fixed.
