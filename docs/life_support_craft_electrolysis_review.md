## 2. Electrolysis components (msg 4)

| Claim | Label |
|---|---|
| ISS OGA: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂; H₂ goes to Sabatier; ~50 % O₂ recovery from CO₂ via Sabatier | **Grounded**. The ~50 % figure is Grok-reported [to verify] and is consistent with the Sabatier route venting methane carbon. |
| Electrolysis as a peak, backup and H₂-feedstock node, with the biological loop carrying the baseline | **Plausible-untested** design choice |
| Water feed with DI or ion exchange and a silver biocide | **Grounded** (silver biocide is used on Orion and Russian systems) |
| A PEM or alkaline stack sized for a partial load | **Grounded** technology; the sizing is [Channing to supply] |
| Power from the "Tesla-resonant / ambient-scavenging network" | **Plausible-untested**, near field only. A stack needs a low-voltage, high-current DC rectifier. |
| Co–MnO nanocubes on mycelium-derived carbon as a catalyst | **Grounded** at lab scale (verified in the living_habitat doc); in flight it is **Speculative** |
| Rotary or membrane gas separators; H₂ to Sabatier, microfluidic CO₂ electrolysis or venting | **Grounded** in kind |
| O₂ delivered through the phi-spiral ducting | **Speculative** (no φ benefit shown) |
| Shared sensors (DO, CO₂, humidity, conductivity); logic that prefers the biological side | **Plausible-untested** |
| Limitations: power and clean-water cost; H₂ leak safety; mycelium catalysts not flight-ready; >75–90 % closure needs advanced CO₂ reduction or photosynthesis | **Grounded**. The 75–90 % range is Grok-reported [to verify]. |

**Ledger rule:** net regenerative gain is not claimed until the biological loop is shown to reduce the electrolyzer's power and water burden. Grok stated this too.
