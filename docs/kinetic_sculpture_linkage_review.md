**Rebuild, Part 2: φ-tuned waves (Grok's message 10 series 1, φ, φ², φ³, φ⁴ × base).**
- Grok's series is correct arithmetic (φ powers; φ² = φ + 1). It is not a physical result.
- In the rebuild, a three-wave skin with ratios 1:φ:φ² reaches ≥ 90 % of its maximum peak for **17.1 %** of the time, against **15.8 %** for 1:2:3. So φ is no special advantage, consistent with the repo's φ nulls.
- The real difference is that the integer skin repeats every turn of the crank, while the φ skin never repeats (quasi-periodic). For a sculpture that is an aesthetic choice. For a walker it means no fixed sync between gait and skin.
- **Grok's "phi-tuned resonances" are Symbolic.**

| Claim | Check | Label |

| Manta, octopus and crab-style explorers; "they made a crab type prototype" | Real precedents: the Korean Crabster CR200 seabed robot (KIOST, 2014); soft octopus robots (e.g., Octobot, *Nature* **536**, 451 (2016)); manta-ray soft robots. | **Grounded** (precedents) |

| "Weak magnetic containment in 60ish degree arcs that should create perpetual motion, right?" | **No.** Grok answers correctly: "True closed-system perpetual motion is impossible". A static magnetic field is conservative; no arrangement of arcs gives net work per cycle (Earnshaw also forbids static stable trapping of ordinary magnets). "60° = phi-friendly" is wrong: 60° is hexagonal symmetry, not φ (φ appears in 72°/36° pentagonal angles). | **Corrected** |

| Tuned mass dampers (Taipei 101, ~660 t); "mass ratios ~1/φ" | TMDs are real. Optimal tuning follows Den Hartog (f_opt = 1/(1 + μ), with mass ratio μ usually 1–5 %), not 1/φ (≈ 62 %). | **Corrected** |
