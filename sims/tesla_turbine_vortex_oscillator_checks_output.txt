== 3. Single-mass frequency and the coupled pair (msgs 10, 12) ==
f = sqrt(k/m1)/2pi = 7.12 Hz (Grok 7.1 Hz; correct for one mass)
Linear normal modes with k_ground = k_coupling = 1000 N/m: 5.19, 12.60 Hz
-> A 7.1 Hz drive sits between the two normal modes (5.2 and 12.6 Hz), not on a resonance of the coupled pair.
F_amp 10.0 N at  7.1 Hz: peak-to-peak x1 1.29 cm, x2 2.79 cm, mean x1 -0.028 mm, drive power 1.18 W
F_amp 10.0 N at  5.2 Hz: peak-to-peak x1 3.98 cm, x2 5.68 cm, mean x1 +0.043 mm, drive power 3.23 W
F_amp 10.0 N at 12.6 Hz: peak-to-peak x1 2.35 cm, x2 2.48 cm, mean x1 -0.061 mm, drive power 4.58 W
F_amp 0.5 N (EHD scale) at 7.1 Hz: peak-to-peak x1 0.64 mm, x2 1.39 mm, mean x1 -0.0014 mm
-> Amplitudes are mm-to-cm and set by drive and damping; the mean offset is ~0 (the force law is odd in x).
   Grok's 'final positions stabilize near ~0.01 m offset due to drive' does not follow from its own model.
   All the motion is paid for by the drive: the average drive power equals the damping loss.

== 4. 'Hyperbolic' vortex (msg 20) ==
theta   0.0 rad: r = 2.0000 ; r*theta = 0.000
theta   5.0 rad: r = 1.2131 ; r*theta = 6.065
theta  10.0 rad: r = 0.7358 ; r*theta = 7.358
theta  20.0 rad: r = 0.2707 ; r*theta = 5.413
-> A free vortex plus a 1/r sink gives a LOGARITHMIC spiral, r = r0*exp(-c*theta/k), at a constant angle;
   r*theta is not constant, so the paths are not hyperbolic spirals. (A log spiral is golden only for one
   specific c/k; nothing in the model selects it.)
